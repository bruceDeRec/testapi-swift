# Theme and sound: the sonic identity of *Many Hands*

*The music and sound brief for the DeRec Alliance community podcast. It's written for Bruce and Facundo, and for whoever composes the music. It builds on `season/FORMAT.md`, which sets the show name *Many Hands* [HOST GATE], the run of show, and the rule of no music under talk. Every choice comes with its reason. **[HOST GATE]** marks something that needs the hosts' sign-off. **[VERIFY]** marks something to check before relying on it. The full list of both is at the end.*

---

*Timings in this document were reconciled with `intro/COLD-OPEN-STORYBOARD.md`, which owns every frame number (DECISIONS R5).*

## The sound on one page

- **The theme is five seconds long, and it plays the show's proverb without words.** Its rhythm is "*Ma-ny hands make light work*". A vibraphone plays the first half and a nylon-string guitar answers with the second. They land together on one note, and a group of real people claps once.
- **Key:** D major. **Tempo:** 96 BPM. **Meter:** 4/4. **Length:** two bars, then one final chord on the downbeat at exactly **5.00s**, which is the cut to the hosts. At 96 BPM every beat falls on a whole frame at 24 fps (15 frames).
- **Chords:** D(add9) → E/D → Gmaj7 → Gm6 → D(add9). It lifts, then lands warmly. There is no dominant chord anywhere.
- **The hook:** 5̂ 6̂ 1̂ · 6̂ ♯4̂ · 5̂ … 3̂, which is **A4 B4 D5 · B4 G♯4 · A4 … F♯5**.
- **The sound:** mid-century jazz instruments (vibraphone, nylon guitar, Rhodes electric piano, upright bass) plus one warm analog synth whose filter opens like a sunrise. That gives classic structure, modern light, and fun in the details: the brief's "beautifully printed book about tomorrow", in sound.
- **The whole family is built from the same notes and the same proverb.** The logo sting is "*Ma-ny hands*". The outro tag is "*make light work*", played straight after the hosts' split sign-off. The chapter bump is the two-note "*light work*" figure. The myth-buster motif is the hook turned into a question.
- **The music never changes, but one sound does.** Each episode gets one custom sound effect for the mascot's activity, always on the same beat. The ritual stays fixed and the surprise gets a sound of its own.
- **No music under talk, ever**, as FORMAT sets out. Music and voice only overlap where a tail has died away before anyone's words land. Moments of honesty get complete silence (room tone only), with a one-second margin on either side.
- **Masters:** YouTube at −14 LUFS integrated, the podcast feed at −16 LUFS, both at or below −1.0 dBTP.
- **My recommendation: commission a human composer (about $6,000 all-in, with exclusive rights, stems and live players), and ship no AI-generated music.** Library music is a stopgap. AI generation is documented below with prompts, and I recommend against shipping it. [HOST GATE]

## 1. The concept in plain English

It should feel like stepping out onto a sunny terrace with a friend.

It starts mid-motion. A bright, glassy vibraphone plays three rising notes, as if reaching for something, the way Bruce and Facundo reach toward the orbit of light on the cover. A warm nylon-string guitar answers. On the unheard word "work", both instruments land on the same note while a group of real people claps once. The harmony warms, two identical soft plinks sound, and as three stones light up together the home chord blooms with a single bronze glint. On the fifth second, a last chord rings out as we cut to the two hosts, already mid-smile.

Three ideas carry it:

1. **Two voices, one tune.** One instrument asks, the other answers, and they finish together. That's Bruce, the spark, and Facundo, the steady hand. The warmth between them is the product, so the theme is a conversation too.
2. **The proverb, hidden in plain sight.** The melody has the exact rhythm of "*many hands make light work*". Nobody sings it, but regulars will start to hear the words anyway, and the hosts finish the proverb in their sign-off every episode.
3. **Many hands, literally.** The clap on "work" is recorded by real people from the community: the hosts, contributors and friends [HOST GATE]. The theme of a trust show has real human hands in it, and the description can say so.

**It's relaxing** because nothing in it pushes. There's no drum kit, no build, and no chord straining to resolve. **It's uplifting** because the harmony lifts at the start and the final note reaches up. **It's fun** because it bounces, snaps and claps. **It's stylish** because the chords are grown-up jazz colours, not nursery-rhyme triads. **It's slightly futuristic** because of one warm synth and one bright, unexpected note: the raised fourth that film composers reach for when they score flight and wonder.

## 2. The theme in musician's terms

### The spec

| | Choice | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Key** | D major, with a Lydian G♯ in bar 1 and a borrowed B♭ in bar 2 | D suits the open strings, so the guitar and upright bass ring. The hook sits at A4–F♯5 (440–740 Hz), the band a phone speaker reproduces best. |
| **Tempo** | 96 BPM | "Unhurried, but never slow." Below about 85 BPM a cue reads as a ballad (sleepy); above about 110 it reads as hype. 96 is a nod of the head. At 24 fps, one beat is 0.625s, or exactly 15 frames, and two bars last exactly 5.00s. |
| **Meter and feel** | 4/4. Straight eighth notes in the melody. Shaker in sixteenths, swung 56% | The straight melody stays crisp and classic, and the lightly swung shaker gives the relaxed lilt. |
| **Form** | Two bars, then a final downbeat (the "button") at 5.00s, with a tail ringing to 6.50s | The button is the cut to the wide two-shot, the "landing" in FORMAT §5. |
| **Harmonic rhythm** | Bar 1: one chord every two beats (1.25s). Bar 2: Gmaj7 and Gm6 one beat each (0.625s), then home from beat 3 (3.75s) for two beats, then the button at 5.00s. | Slow enough to feel calm, fast enough to feel like a journey in five seconds. Bar two quickens under the two plinks, so home lands exactly as the stones light. |

**In Roman numerals:** I – II/I – IV – iv – I.

**Voice leading:** in bar 1 the inner voices rise by whole steps (F♯→G♯, A→B). In bar 2 they fall by step on each beat, arriving home on beat 3 (G♯→F♯→E, B→B♭→A, E→D). They lift, then exhale.

### The hook

| Hidden syllable | MA- | -ny | HANDS | *(rest)* | make | LIGHT | WORK | *(button)* |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Scale degree** | 5̂ | 6̂ | 1̂ (upper) | – | 6̂ | ♯4̂ | 5̂ | 3̂ (upper) |
| **Note** | A4 | B4 | D5 | – | B4 | G♯4 | A4 | F♯5 |
| **Rhythm** | eighth | eighth | quarter | eighth rest | eighth | quarter | whole note (all of bar 2) | rings 1.5s |
| **Starts** | 0.00s | 0.31s | 0.63s | 1.25s | 1.56s | 1.88s | 2.50s | 5.00s |
| **Frame (24 fps)** | 0 | 7.5 | 15 | 30 | 37.5 | 45 | 60 | 120 |
| **Played by** | vibes | vibes | vibes | – | guitar | guitar | vibes + guitar | vibes (the guitar rolls the chord) |

On one line: `| A4 B4 D5 (rest) B4 G♯4 | A4 ——————— | F♯5 (ring) |`, with the rhythm `| ♪ ♪ ♩ 𝄾 ♪ ♩ | 𝅝 | 𝅝 |`.

**The shape.** Three notes reach up (5̂–6̂–1̂). The line falls to the one surprising note, ♯4̂. Over E/D that note is the chord's third, so it sounds bright rather than wrong. It then rises by a half step into the long 5̂, and at the very end it leaps up a sixth to 3̂ over the home chord. Every memorable hook has one surprise, and here the G♯ is it. It also does the "slightly futuristic" work on its own.

The long A4 in bar 2 is approached from both sides: the G♯ lifts into it from below, and the B♭ in the Gm6 sighs toward it from above. The melody holds still through the whole ritual, and the chords shift warmly beneath that one held note until home arrives. The eye has plenty to do at that moment, so the ear isn't asked for more.

Some eighth notes fall between frames. The picture only ever hits beats, which are always whole frames.

### The one sound that changes every episode

The music is identical in every episode, down to the sample. What changes is **one sound effect for the mascot's activity**: a kayak paddle's splash (the lake image), a bicycle bell (the vineyard image), the click of a telescope's focus knob (the observatory image), a cork popping.

- **It lands on a beat in bar one:** frame 15, 30 or 45 (0.63s, 1.25s or 1.88s). Frame 30 is the default. Bar two belongs to the ritual, and the clap on frame 60 lands with the pocket pat.
- **It lasts 0.50s at most** and sits at least 6 dB under the hook.
- **Anything with pitch is tuned to D major pentatonic** (D E F♯ A B), so it never clashes.
- **It's a real recording or library sound effect, never generated.** It's never a voice, a word, money, a lock or an alarm (see "What the show never sounds like", §4).
- **The End Card reprise gets its twin:** the resolved version of the same sound (the fish lands, the cork pours), placed in a gap in the outtake's talk or laughter, or left out.

**Why:** FORMAT keeps the activity secret until the bumper. Regulars will listen for this one sound. The ritual stays intact and the surprise gets a sound of its own.

### One alternate, because it's genuinely different

**"Table taps."** The same rhythm played entirely as body percussion by the hosts and the community (snaps, claps, knuckle taps on the set's table, a glass token set down on marble), landing on a single vibraphone chord at 5.00s. It's rhythm-first, almost free and entirely human. It loses because it's fun without being relaxing, it's not futuristic at all, and it can't carry a family of cues. Keep it in your back pocket only if the budget is zero.

## 3. Why it feels relaxed and uplifted

**Relaxed**

- **No dominant chord.** There's no A7 and no C♯ leading note pulling toward home, so there's no tension waiting to resolve. The ending is plagal (iv→I), the "amen" cadence: it arrives without pushing.
- **A pedal point.** Through bar 1 the bass holds D while the harmony moves above it. The ear hears "we haven't left home" even as the colour brightens.
- **The inner voices fall by step through bar 2.** An exhale.
- **Tempo and touch.** 96 BPM, soft attacks (yarn mallets, felt, fingers, no sticks), nothing boosted above about 8 kHz, and one beat of stillness before the last chord.

**Uplifted**

- **The Lydian II.** The E/D chord raises the fourth degree to G♯. That raised fourth is the most reliably hopeful, airborne colour in Western harmony, and film scores use it for flight and wonder. It is the "slightly futuristic" feeling in a single note.
- **The inner voices rise by step through bar 1.**
- **The melody opens by reaching up (5̂–6̂–1̂) and ends by leaping up a sixth to 3̂.** Ending on 3̂ instead of 1̂ keeps the door open. It's resolved, but it smiles and hands over to people.

**The two colours together.** The G♯ is brighter than plain major, and the B♭ is warmer and darker than plain major. The theme travels from one to the other around a stable D, which is "relaxed and uplifted" put into harmony.

**Why it survives the hundredth hearing**

- **It's short.** Fatigue needs time to build.
- **One surprise (G♯) and one note of depth (the borrowed B♭).** Pure major-key sweetness cloys. One touch of wistfulness is the detail regulars keep noticing.
- **No words, no voice.** Words wear out fastest, and the mascot never speaks anyway.
- **It's no louder than the talk that follows** (§5). Intros that are louder than the show are the ones people learn to hate.

### Sound design signatures

These three sounds represent the protocol in graphics. Five principles come first:

1. **Use the real sound wherever there is one.** On set, physical things make their own sound: the amber glass tokens on the table and the Jargon Jar. Never sweeten or replace a real action with a designed sound. These signatures are for graphics, animation and graphics in the Shorts only.
2. **A sound confirms; it never claims.** A designed sound plays only when the picture shows the thing happening. On a screen recording of real software, a signature plays only in sync with the real on-screen event. It never stands in for something the viewer can't see.
3. **Every share makes the same sound.** A share isn't a piece of the secret: one share reveals nothing, not even one letter (FORMAT's first myth). If each share had its own note and together they played the tune, the sound design would teach exactly the myth the show busts. So every share sounds identical, and only its position in the stereo field changes.
4. **Glass, wood and felt. Never metal, never electronics.** A coin landing on a table is exactly the sound to avoid. No beeps.
5. **Recurring bits get no sound effects, and neither does honesty.** Percent Right, the Jargon Jar tally and the IOU card are carried by the talk. A "ding" on an IOU payoff would turn integrity into a gag.

|---|---|
| 0.00s–0.12s | A glass token sliding 2–3 cm across honed marble (a real recording), high-passed at 800 Hz |
| 0.12s | Contact: glass meeting stone, a small "tk" |
| 0.12s–0.45s | Vibraphone A5 (880 Hz), yarn mallet, motor off, 12 dB under the contact, damped at 0.40s, so the token rings faintly |

**Level:** momentary max −20 LUFS.
**Why this sound:** it echoes the café image, where the hosts place amber gems on marble. That's the show's most useful metaphor image, and it matches the tokens Facundo uses on the table.

#### S02 A helper's quiet daily check-in: "knock, and 'here'" (1.20s at most)

| Time | Layer |
|---|---|
| 0.00s and 0.14s | Two soft knuckle taps on a wooden tabletop (a real recording, ideally of the set's own table) |
| 0.40s onward | One reply for each helper shown: a soft Rhodes D5 (587 Hz), the same pitch every time, 0.08–0.15s apart. Each reply sounds a little farther away than the last (1 dB quieter, with a little more room sound), panned to that helper's position. The last reply has rung out by 1.20s. |

**Why this sound:** a neighbour's knock is the most homely way to say "just checking you're OK". The replies are musical and warm: "still here". This is the quietest sound in the show, at a momentary max of −22 LUFS.
**Never:** radar or sonar pings, heartbeat monitors, notification dings, camera-shutter clicks, scanning sweeps or beep patterns. They suggest surveillance, a hospital or an alarm. In a day-by-day animation, play it once per "day", and never for more than three days in a row: a repeating check-in turns into a ticking clock.
**Accuracy:** this pictures what brief §5 describes, the owner's device checking that each helper's device still holds its share. It must not suggest that helpers talk to each other. The replies go only to the owner, in the centre of the stereo field.

#### S03 Recovery completes: "home" (2.00s at most after the last share arrives)

| Step | Layer |
|---|---|
| The shares return | S01's contact sound and A5 ring, panned from the sides to the centre, 0.15–0.25s apart, as many as the picture shows. Nothing else plays: no rising pitch, no riser, no build. |
| +0.12s | Silence: a breath. |
| The bloom | The bumper's final chord, D(add9) (Rhodes E3 A3 D4 F♯4, vibes F♯5, pad), faded in over 0.15s rather than struck, plus the crotale A6 glint. |
| Up to +2.00s | It rings out to silence. |

**Why this sound:** below the threshold, shares reveal nothing, and at the threshold the whole secret comes back at once. The sound behaves the same way. The returning shares are identical ticks that build toward nothing, and then the whole chord appears at once, not note by note. It's the theme's home chord because recovery means getting back to where you were.
**Must not:** fanfares, brass, cymbal crashes, drum hits, risers, "level up" sounds, jackpots, cash registers, lock clicks, vault doors or applause. It should sound like the lights coming on in your own kitchen, not like winning something. That's non-negotiable 2 in sound: no triumph, no promise of absolute safety.
**Level:** short-term max −18 LUFS. Place it in a gap in the narration, and hold the shot for 1.5s so the moment lands. Never play it over a failed or partial demo, and never inside or next to Where It Stops.

### What the show never sounds like

- **Ukulele, whistling, a glockenspiel lead, stock clap tracks, "inspiring corporate piano" builds.** The explainer-video cliché.
- **Trailer "braams", risers, whooshes, drops, stadium drums.** Hype reads as anxiety.
- **Coins, cash registers, slot machines, "ka-ching".** Money.
- **Locks, vault doors, jangling keys, shield whooshes, alarms, sirens.** Fear-selling, and promises of safety.
- **Glitches, data beeps, modem noise, clattering keyboards, "hacker" drones.** The crypto and cyber clichés: non-negotiable 6, in sound.
- **Vocals, lyrics, vocoders, robot voices, a voice for the mascot.**
- **Minor-key "danger" beds.**

## 5. Bed policy

### Under the conversation: never

From The Question to Next Time, no music plays under anyone speaking. This is FORMAT's rule, and I'd hold it hard:

- **Grandma.** Age-related hearing loss mostly takes the high frequencies where consonants live, and it makes speech far harder to follow against any background sound. A bed that sounds "subtle" to a 35-year-old editor can cost a 70-year-old whole words.
- **The conversation is the product.** A music bed tells the viewer "this is produced content". Silence around two voices tells them "these are two people talking".
- **Beds make shows sound alike.** Leaving the talk dry is part of the identity.

### Where music and voice do meet

Assume dialogue sits at about −14 LUFS short-term in the YouTube master.

| Moment | What plays | Level relative to dialogue | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Landing** (0:05–0:07) | The bumper's final chord, dying away | It decays from about 1 LU below dialogue level to silence, and must be at least 20 LU below Bruce's first word | Gone by 6.50s. If Bruce's first word comes before 6.50s, use the `ALT-TAILSHORT` version, which is gone by 5.75s. |
| **Segment tabs** | M03 or M05 | Momentary max −16 LUFS | Played in the clear, finishing before Bruce names the segment. |
| **Verdicts** | The stamps | Momentary max −17 LUFS | In the breath after the verdict word. The editor may open up a pause of up to 10 frames. |
| **Graphics, animation, Hands On overlays** | S01–S03 | −20 to −22 LUFS (S03 at −18) | In gaps, never under a word. The editor may open up a gap of up to 12 frames. |
| **Sign-off into the End Card** | M04, then the outtake's own sound | Short-term max −16 LUFS | Starts after "—light work." and finishes before the outtake's first word. |
| **Light Work Shorts** | M06 only | Momentary max −15 LUFS | The dialogue has already ended, and it's silent by the loop frame. No bed in Shorts either. |
| **Trailer, Premiere** | M07, M02 | The music alone at −15 LUFS | The hosts speak before or after the music, never over it. |

### The Still: complete silence for honesty

Whenever anyone is saying what recovery doesn't, can't or won't do, there's no music and no designed sound. It's the same mechanical rule FORMAT sets for the mascot. That covers:

- **Where It Stops**, from 1.0s before "So where does it stop?" to 1.0s after Bruce's reaction line. No tab sting, no sound on the text band, and no tail from the previous segment ringing into it. Afterwards, leave at least 1.0s of room tone before the Raised Hands bump, so the music's return doesn't sound like relief.
- Every **"True, but…"** verdict: the dry stamp, then nothing.
- Any **Raised Hands answer that turns into a limit.**
- Any **failed or partial demo.**
- **IOU card payoffs and on-air corrections.**

Silence means the set's own room tone (record 30 seconds at every session), never digital silence, which sounds like the audio has dropped out.

### Episode masters

| | YouTube | Podcast feed (Apple Podcasts, and Spotify via RSS) |

| **Integrated loudness** | −14 LUFS (±0.5) | −16 LUFS (±1) |

**Why −14 for YouTube:** YouTube turns uploads louder than about −14 LUFS down, and doesn't turn quieter ones up [VERIFY: YouTube doesn't publish the figure; check "Stats for nerds" → content loudness on the pilot upload]. A −16 master on YouTube just plays quieter than everything around it, so older viewers turn it up and then get blasted by the next ad.
**Why −16 for the feed:** it's Apple's published recommendation (−16 LKFS ±1, true peak no higher than −1 dBFS, measured per BS.1770-5). Spotify normalises to −14 in both directions, so a −16 file is fine there too.

It's the same mix with two gain settings, not two mixes. Measure to ITU-R BS.1770 / EBU R128. Both masters must survive being folded down to mono (phone speakers, smart speakers) with the hook still recognisable.

### What the composer delivers

- **WAV, 48 kHz / 24-bit, stereo** (the S01 and S02 layers in mono), not normalised, peaks at or below −1.0 dBTP.
- **Sample-accurate timing.** Every cue starts on its first transient at sample 0, with no pre-roll. M01's button lands at **sample 240,000** (5.000s, frame 120 at 24 fps), and M01 ends at 6.500s (sample 312,000).

- **M01 stems**, which must null against the mix when summed at unity gain:

- **S01–S03:** every layer separately (the recorded sound and the tonal ring) as well as the finished sound, so the editor can pan each share.

## 8. How to tell if it's right, by ear

Play the finished bumper, then run these seven tests. Any failure sends it back.

1. **The morning hum.** Hear it three times today. Can you hum it tomorrow morning? If not, the hook is too weak.
2. **Twenty loops.** Play it twenty times in a row, which takes under two minutes. Whatever annoys you by the twentieth time comes out.
3. **The kitchen phone.** Play it on a phone speaker at half volume from across a kitchen. The tune must still be recognisable. If all that carries is a vague shimmer, the mix is wrong.
4. **The stranger.** Play it once to someone over 70 who has never heard of the show, and ask, "What kind of programme do you think this is?" A pass is friendly, calm, hopeful, or something about tomorrow. A fail is a bank, an advert, a children's show, a video game or a news bulletin.
5. **The handoff.** Play the bumper straight into Bruce's first line. His voice should feel like the next beat, not an interruption. If you reached for the volume at the join, it fails.
6. **Eyes shut.** Do you hear money, a lock, an alarm, a spaceship or a robot? Any one of those is a fail.
7. **The Still.** Play a chapter bump, a segment, then Where It Stops in silence. The silence should feel deliberate and calm, not broken. If it sounds as if the audio dropped out, the room tone is missing.
