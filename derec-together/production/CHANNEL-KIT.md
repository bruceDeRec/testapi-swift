*How* Many Hands *gets found, and stays trustworthy once it is, on the existing DeRec Alliance YouTube channel. Written for Bruce Longley and Facundo La Rocca. It covers the channel trailer, channel art, playlists, thumbnails, titles, descriptions, Light Work, cross-posting, the first 90 days, comment moderation, and the neutrality and AI lines. It follows `DECISIONS.md` first; then `intro/COLD-OPEN-STORYBOARD.md` for every frame number; `season/SEASON-ONE.md` for titles, order and dates; `mascot/CHARACTER-BIBLE.md` for Tuck and Pip; `brand/BRAND-KIT.md` for colour and type; and `production/PLAYBOOK.md` for delivery, naming and gates. ## The kit on one page

- **The channel stays "DeRec Alliance".** The show lives on it as a named series. The channel name is the imprint, and FORMAT's four naming fixes depend on it.
- **Channel trailer, TR01, 47.50s:** the Episode 01 bumper exactly as it airs, Bruce's question, Facundo's answer, ten wordless seconds of the two of them, the promise on a paper card, and the title card. Music never plays under a word. Published Wednesday 4 November, as the playbook sets.

- **Playlists:** the fixed podcast playlist, *Start here*, *Light Work*, and one playlist per pillar.
- **Thumbnails:** one paper page, top left, in one face; the two hosts on the right; one printed-to-lit thread. The only things that change are the words and the moment. The lines are measured, not counted: the brand kit's "10 characters a line" overflows the page.
- **Every description and every pinned comment carries the same line:** *we will never message you privately.*
- **Light Work** has three formats: Clip, Answer and Banked. Each ends on the storyboard's 1.0s stamp, rendered once as a native vertical pass.
- **Cross-posting:** the audio feed via RSS, the Alliance website, and the Alliance's LinkedIn and X. No TikTok or Instagram until the day-90 review. Every new surface is another comment section to defend.
- **The first 90 days** (11 November 2026 to 8 February 2027): seven flagships and thirteen Shorts. Expect hundreds of views an episode, not thousands. Judge the show on retention and on answers to the pinned question.
- **Moderation assumes the scam, not the risk of it.** Blocked words, links held, a strict hold, sweeps within four hours of any upload, one reporting path, and a log.

### Where this kit departs from the other documents

The storyboard's crop (a full-height 9:16 window of the pocket shot) puts the stones at about 82% of frame height. That is inside the band the Shorts app covers with the title and channel name; the brand kit's safe area ends at 75%. §7 below gives the fix.

4. **The pinned comment merges three sources:** Episode 01's question, word for word, from the script; the playbook's consent wording; and the playbook's scam line.
5. **Episode 01's Raised Hands never comes from the trailer's comments** (the script agrees): the trailer publishes on 4 November, after the 20 October recording. The trailer's thread feeds the clinic and the Light Work bank instead.
6. **Moderation costs about 2 more editor hours a fortnight** than the playbook's §9 budget. The playbook's blocked-words list is kept and extended (§10).

### The avatar: stays the Alliance's own

The bible recommends keeping the Alliance's avatar. **Follow it.** Here's why:

1. **The avatar is the face on every reply the channel writes.** Bruce answers comments from the channel account (PLAYBOOK §7). Many of those replies are scam warnings or "recovery can't do that". FORMAT §7 is mechanical: *whenever anyone is saying that recovery doesn't, can't or won't do something, the mascot is off screen.* A Tuck avatar would break that rule every day, in the one place the editor can't police it.
2. **The avatar is the anti-impersonation anchor.** Viewers who already know the Alliance's mark have a reference point. Changing it in launch week, when scam replies start, makes the fakes easier. A cartoon otter is also far easier to copy convincingly than an established mark.
3. **The channel is the Alliance's, not the show's.** Tuck is the show's character, not the Alliance's logo. Putting her in the avatar would brand every Alliance video with the show, and it would put a cute mascot beside member-related content, which FORMAT §7 rules out.
4. **Grandma test at 40 px.** The comment avatar renders at about 24–40 px. At that size the Alliance mark's job is to be recognised, not charming.

**What the Alliance should check on its own avatar** **[VERIFY]**:
- If it's the shield with "PROTECTED BY DeRec", that line is unreadable at avatar size, and it reads as a protection claim (brand kit §3). The plain mark is better. That is the Alliance's decision **[HOST GATE]**.
- Upload 800 × 800 px, under 4 MB **[VERIFY]**. Check it in greyscale at 98 px and 24 px.
- **No change to the avatar from 4 November to 8 February.** Stability is a security feature (§10).

The bible's Tuck avatar and emoji set (rung 1) is still made. It is used *inside* community posts and on stickers, never as the channel's picture.

## 4. The thumbnail system

### The job

A row of *Many Hands* thumbnails has to read as one series from across the room, without the mascot (FORMAT §7 keeps Tuck and Pip off thumbnails), without an episode number and without a logo (brand kit §6). **The series is recognised by what never changes.** So almost nothing changes.

### What never changes (the constants)

1. **One page, one place.** A Paper `#F7F3E6` slip at x 32, y 32, 512 px wide, with an 8 px Jade spine on the left, a 6 px radius and the lift shadow. Always top left. Its height follows the lines:
   - 2 lines: 360 px (50% of H, the brand kit's floor);
   - 3 lines: 472 px.
2. **One voice.** The brand kit's thumb instance (`MH Display Thumb`: Fraunces opsz 144, wght 720, SOFT 100, WONK 0), −20 tracking, **Ink only**, 112 px, leading 1.0, 48 px inner padding. No second size, no second colour, no italic.
3. **Two friends, same sides, same clothes.** Bruce on the left, Facundo on the right, both inside x 544–1280, each face at least 158 px tall (22% of H). The cream jacket beside the olive overshirt becomes the series' silhouette in a row. That's why the brand kit's wardrobe rule matters here too.
4. **One grade.** `MH_Daylight_709_v1` on a real frame from that session (PLAYBOOK §7). Never an AI likeness, never a retouched face (PLAYBOOK §10).
5. **One thread.**
   - It starts as a printed 3 px Brass line under the last line of words, from a printed node at the text's left edge.
   - It leaves the page's right edge at x 544 and turns lit (Gilt, 3 px, with the kit's glow) over the photograph.
   - It sags 4–10%, and ends in one lit node on the hosts' hands or on the object they share.
   - It never crosses a face, and stays below the chin line.
6. **The empty corners.** Top right, 120 × 120 px (YouTube's hover icons), and bottom right, 230 × 100 px (the duration), hold no face and no type.

### What changes (the only two variables)

1. **The words:** 2–4 words, in the viewer's voice, ending in "?". They are a shorthand of the question, not a copy of the title.
2. **The moment:** a real, unstaged reaction with a real object between them. Keep a row log, and never run the same expression type (laugh, thinking, deadpan, surprise) twice in a row.

**Nothing else varies.** No pillar colours, no seasonal decorations, no holiday props, no "NEW", no arrows, no circles, no emoji, no capitals, no second line of small text, no shield, no logo, no mascot, no Pip (brand kit §6, bible).

### The word rules

- **Fit is measured, not counted.**
  - The template's text box is 408 px wide (512 − 8 spine − 48 − 48) and never auto-shrinks.
  - A line that overflows at 112 px drops to 104 px.
  - If it still overflows, rewrite the words. Never go below 104 px.
  - Measured with the instance, "tonight?" is 390 px and "the lake?" is 414 px, so the practical ceiling at 112 px is about 8 lowercase characters.
- **No "DeRec"** unless the episode's question is about DeRec (06, 08). To a stranger it's an unknown word taking up a line.
- **No fear words:** hack, hacked, stolen, scam, danger, warning, safe, secure. The show never sells fear or promises safety (non-negotiable 2).
- **Don't repeat the title's first words.** The title is right under the thumbnail, so the page should add to it.
- **Plain question words** (who, what, can, how) beat nouns.

## 10. Comment moderation

### The threat, stated plainly

A show about getting your things back, on a channel with "DeRec" in its name, **will** get scam replies. The pattern is always the same:

- An account whose display name and avatar copy "DeRec Alliance", "Bruce Longley" or "Facundo", or a "support" name.
- It replies under a real viewer's worried comment: *"Thanks for reaching out, message me on WhatsApp/Telegram"*, or it posts a phone number or an email.
- Or it posts a staged testimony: *"I lost my wallet and [name] on Telegram recovered it for me."*
- The goal is to move the victim somewhere private and take their recovery phrase or their money.

This is the scam Where It Stops says recovery doesn't stop. The show must not host it for a minute longer than it takes to remove it.

### Prevention: the settings

Set these on Wed 14 October in YouTube Studio → Settings → Community (and the live chat settings). **[VERIFY]** every setting name against the current Studio.

| Setting | Value | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Comments on new videos | On, with **"Hold potentially inappropriate comments for review"** at its **strictest level** | Catches most bot replies before anyone sees them |
| **Block links** | On | Comments with URLs or hashtags are held. Scammers need a way out; legitimate viewers rarely post links. |
| **Blocked words** | The list below | Held for review, not deleted, so a real victim's plea isn't lost |
| Moderators | Bruce, the editor, and Facundo (for the Premieres he joins) | Three people who can remove and hide |
| Approved users | Empty | Nobody bypasses the filters, including the hosts' personal accounts |
| Premiere and live chat | Hold potentially inappropriate messages on; blocked words applied **[VERIFY]** that they apply to chat; slow mode 10 seconds; subscribers-only **off** (first-time viewers are the audience) | Bots flood the chat in the first minutes |
| Pinned chat message | Bruce's first chat message, pinned at 10:45: *"Hi, Bruce here, from the DeRec Alliance channel. We'll never message you privately or ask for a password, phrase or key. Anyone offering to recover something for you is running a scam."* | The Premiere is where scam accounts first appear |
| Upload defaults | The description template (§6), category Education, comments held as above | So a rushed upload can't ship with defaults |

**Blocked words.** The playbook's ten are kept, and this list extends them. Entries are case-insensitive. **[VERIFY]** how YouTube matches partial words and symbols.

```
whatsapp, whats app, what's app, telegram, t.me, wa.me, signal me, text me, message me, dm me, inbox me, write to him, write to her, contact him, contact her, reach him, reach her, recovery expert, recovery agent, recovery specialist, recovery company, fund recovery, funds recovery, asset recovery, crypto recovery, wallet recovery, recover your wallet, recover your funds, recover your account, retrieve your, hacker, ethical hacker, hack back, account manager, customer support, support team, help desk, helpdesk, technician, mentor, broker, trader, investment, profit, forex, signals, @gmail, gmail.com, @outlook, @yahoo, @hotmail, @proton, +1, +44, +234, +61, +91, seed phrase, recovery phrase, secret phrase, private key, mnemonic, 12 words, 24 words, validate, sync your wallet, rectify, walletconnect, airdrop, giveaway, claim your, testimony, got my money back, helped me recover, I was scammed
```

- **Deliberately not blocked:** "recover", "recovery", "hack" and "scam" on their own. They're the show's own subject, and holding every comment that uses them would bury the real conversation.
- "I was scammed" **is** blocked, so that a real victim's comment is held where scam bots can't see it. Bruce answers it before it goes public (below).
- Every new contact handle, number pattern or misspelling an impostor uses is added the same day ("te1egram", "wh@tsapp").

**The pinned line** goes in every pinned comment, every description, the About text and the Premiere chat, in the words set in §6:

> *We will never message you privately, and nobody in these comments can recover a wallet or an account for you. We only ever reply from this channel, @[handle], and YouTube highlights its name. Anyone else using our names is not us.*

**The hosts' side**
- Bruce and Facundo **never comment from personal accounts** on the channel (PLAYBOOK §7). The channel account's name is highlighted by YouTube on its own videos, and that highlight is the tell viewers are taught to look for.
- Each host's personal profiles carry *"I'll never DM you about recovery."*
- The channel won't qualify for YouTube's verification badge (100,000 subscribers) **[VERIFY]**. The defence is the handle, the highlight, the pinned line and speed.

### Channel security

A hijacked security-education channel streaming a crypto scam would end the show. It's the most common way YouTube channels are taken over.

1. **The channel is a Brand Account with two owners:** Bruce and one Alliance officer **[VERIFY]** who.
2. **The editor gets a Studio role** (Editor), never an owner's login, and never a shared password.
3. **Every owner and manager account uses two-step verification with a passkey or hardware security key.** Not SMS.
4. **Never open a "sponsorship" or "collaboration" attachment or link** on a machine signed in to the channel. Fake sponsorship offers carrying malware that steals the login session are the standard hijack.
5. **Recovery details on every owner account** are current, and checked at the day-90 review.
6. **If it happens:** YouTube's hijacked-channel help flow **[VERIFY]** the current route. Then a plain community post from Bruce once control is back, saying what happened.

### Handling: what to do, every time

**Sweeps**
- Within **4 hours** during the first 48 hours after any upload: roughly 11:30, 14:00 and 18:00 ET on Premiere day and Short day, and 09:00 and 18:00 the next day.
- Within **24 hours** after that: the editor on weekday mornings, Bruce on weekends. About 10 minutes each.
- Each sweep checks the **Held for review** and **Likely spam** tabs, and the newest replies under the pinned comment. That's where impostors reply.

**An impostor reply** (anyone posing as the Alliance, a host or "support")
1. **Don't reply to it.** Never engage.
2. **Remove** the comment.
3. **Hide user from channel.** Their future comments and chat messages on the channel go unseen.
4. **Report** the comment as spam or scam, and report the **channel** for impersonation (its channel page → About → Report user → Impersonation) **[VERIFY]** the current route.
5. If it uses a host's own name or photo, **that host also reports it** through YouTube's impersonation form as the person impersonated **[VERIFY]**.
6. **Check who it replied to.** If a real viewer engaged, the channel replies publicly: *"Heads up: the account that replied to you wasn't us, and it's been removed. We never message anyone privately. Please don't contact it."*
7. **Log it** in `MH_CH_ModerationLog_v01.xlsx`: date, video, impostor handle and display name, what it asked for, actions taken, report status, and who handled it.
8. **Add** its contact pattern to the blocked words.

**A plea for help** ("I lost my wallet, can anyone help?")
- If it's held, **approve it and reply in the same sitting**, never approve first and reply later: scam bots reply within minutes.
- The reply:
  > *"We're sorry. We can't recover anything for anyone, and nobody in these comments can. Anyone who offers, or asks you to message them, is running a scam. For an account, start with the company's own 'forgot password' page, reached by typing its address yourself. Please don't post more details here."*
- Never give specifics about someone's setup (PLAYBOOK §7).

**Someone who's already been scammed**
> *"We're so sorry. We can't get anything back, and nobody in these comments can: anyone who offers is running a second scam. If money moved, call your bank's fraud line today. In the US you can report it at reportfraud.ftc.gov and ic3.gov; in the UK, to Action Fraud; elsewhere, your national fraud line. Please don't share any more details here."*

**[VERIFY]** the reporting links, and the bank-fraud-line advice (SEASON-ONE #26), before first use.

**A comment that exposes a secret** (a recovery phrase, a password, a helper's name or phone number)
- **Remove it** at the next sweep.
- YouTube can't notify them privately, and a public reply would point at what they posted. So remove it and say nothing.

**What stays up** (the neutrality rule applied to comments)
- Criticism of the show, the hosts or DeRec.
- Sceptical and hostile-but-civil questions.
- Questions about members.
- Mentions of other projects in passing.
- Honest disagreement is the show's material. Deleting it on a trust show would be worse than hosting it.

**What comes down, whoever posts it**
- Scams and impersonation.
- Links to or promotion of any product, app, wallet, coin or referral code, **including from a member organisation's staff** (non-negotiable 4).
- Price talk and shilling.
- Harassment and hate.
- Anyone's personal data.
- Off-topic spam.

**Hearts and replies**
- The channel hearts answers to the pinned question, not comments naming products. A heart reads as an endorsement.
- Bruce never answers "which one should I use?" with a name: *"We don't pick winners here"*, then what to look for.

**Community warnings.** Post a warning only if an impostor channel, app or website is actively using the DeRec name *and* viewers have been contacted. Make it plain, and never link to the impostor. Otherwise, a warning mainly advertises the fake.
