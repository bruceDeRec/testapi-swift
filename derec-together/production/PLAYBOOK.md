# Many Hands: production playbook

*How the DeRec Alliance community podcast gets recorded, edited, named, reviewed and delivered. Written for Bruce Longley and Facundo La Rocca, and for the editor they hire. It implements `DECISIONS.md`, `season/FORMAT.md`, `brand/BRAND-KIT.md` and `music/THEME-AND-SOUND.md`. Where those documents and this one disagree, `DECISIONS.md` wins first; then the storyboard for any frame number, the character bible for anything Tuck or Pip does, the brand kit for colour and type, and the music document for sound. This playbook owns the rest: gear, the session, the edit, file names, delivery, gates, hours and AI practice. **[HOST GATE]** marks something that needs Bruce's or Facundo's sign-off. **[VERIFY]** marks a fact to check before anyone relies on it. Both are listed at the end.*

---

## The playbook on one page

- **One show, two setups.** Each host owns a *host kit*: camera, lens, lights, mic and recorder. At home it is a complete remote studio. At the table it becomes one of the in-person cameras. The in-person set adds only a two-shot camera, an overhead and a table. You buy one kit and get both setups. [HOST GATE]
- **Remote cuts together as "the spread":** each host is a page of an open book, joined by a thin Paper gutter. The props that need a table get remote twins (§1).
- **Cameras:** Panasonic Lumix S5II bodies, Lumix S 50mm f/1.8 and 35mm f/1.8 lenses, 4K 23.976 in V-Log, 10-bit. An iPhone in Apple Log for the overhead.
- **Light:** "warm people, cool sky." A 5000 K key from the partner's side, a 3800 K sun rim from upstage camera-left, a 6500 K sky bounce, a 2700 K practical. No diffusion filters.
- **Audio:** dynamic mics into 32-bit float recorders. Tier 1 is the Shure MV7+ and Zoom H4essential. Tier 2 is the Shure SM7dB and Sound Devices MixPre-3 II.
- **Remote platform: Riverside,** used for the call, the producer seat and a backup. The masters are recorded locally on each host's camera and recorder.
- **Backups:** every sound and picture exists twice the moment it is recorded. No card is formatted until its contents are verified by checksum on two drives and uploaded to the cloud.
- **Edit:** one hired editor-producer, in **Adobe Premiere Pro**, with the brand kit's locked look `MH_Daylight_709_v1`. Captions are drafted by software and corrected line by line by a person. The show never publishes auto-captions alone.
- **Gates:** Facundo signs the facts twice, once before recording and once on the cut. Each host signs his own likeness. Bruce signs the AI log. Both sign the final cut.
- **Schedule:** record every call-week Tuesday, lock 13 days later, premiere 9 days after that (R+22). There is always one finished episode in the can. [HOST GATE]
- **Hours:** about 33 editor hours, 11 for Bruce and 8.5–11.5 for Facundo per flagship once the show is running. The first episode takes the editor about 60.
- **AI** drafts transcripts and captions, helps assemble rough cuts and helps explore the look. It never touches the hosts' faces or voices, the ritual master or the music, and it never supplies a fact.

### Where this playbook departs from the foundation documents

1. **FORMAT assumes one table.** This playbook plans both setups, and gives the remote version its own composition and props (§1). [HOST GATE, FORMAT sign-off item 11]
2. **FORMAT's grid records episode N+1 eight days before it airs.** This playbook records N+2 and keeps a finished episode in reserve at all times. The loop runs one episode behind as a result (§9). [HOST GATE]
3. **FORMAT asks for two different first lines in the description.** One Small Thing goes first, because Bruce tells viewers on air that it's "the first line of the description". The "*Many Hands* is the DeRec Alliance community podcast…" sentence follows (§7).
4. **The brand kit asks for keys of 120 cm or larger.** This playbook uses a 90 cm dome at about 1 m, which gives the face the same apparent source size as a 120 cm source at 1.3 m and fits a home office. Apparent size is what makes light soft.
5. **Hosts wear in-ear monitors when remote,** never over-ear headphones, so their faces read the same as at the table.

## 1. Two setups, one show

### The recommendation

**Buy two host kits first. Treat remote as the setup every episode from 03 on can use, and the table as the upgrade whenever both hosts are in one city.** [HOST GATE]

- **The exception is the launch pair.** Episode 01 depends on one table. Facundo pockets Bruce's phone at the landing (0:05) and slides it back across the table at 14:04, and Hands On needs four crew hands, one from each edge of camera D. Neither has a remote version. Episodes 01 and 02 record in person, and so does E00, which rehearses E01's setup (§9).
- **Why:** the brief doesn't say the hosts live near each other. A fortnightly show can't depend on travel. A kit that is also half of the in-person set means no money goes on gear only one setup uses.
- **The standard:** a viewer should not be able to tell which setup was used from the light, the colour or the sound. They will see the composition change, and that's fine.
- **The in-person location:** a room with soft furnishings, a window you can black out, and space for a table 140 × 80 cm with 2.5 m in front of it. A host's home or a neutral rented room works. **Never a member company's office or branded space,** because that suggests the show belongs to that member (non-negotiable 3).

### The spread: the remote two-shot

The in-person show's home is the two-shot. The remote show's home is **the spread**: two pages of an open book.

|---|---|

| Gutter | A 12 px Paper `#F7F3E6` band at x 954–966, full height. No shadow, no thread, no motion. It's the fold of the book. |

### Matching two rooms

Both hosts set up from the same **setup card**, a single printed page kept at each desk. It covers:

- **Distances:** lens at seated eye height, 1.4 m from the host's eyes. The pastry board's front edge 55 cm from the lens axis base. The key 1 m from the face.
- **Where the host sits in frame:** Bruce at 40% of frame width, Facundo at 60%. Each has lead room toward the gutter, so his full-frame single is well composed and the spread's crop sits neatly around him.
- **Where the call screen goes:** seen from behind the camera, Bruce's call screen sits just right of his lens, and Facundo's just left, about 20° off axis at eye height. Each host then looks toward the gutter while he listens, and into the lens when he speaks to the audience.
- **Which side the light comes from:** the key comes from the side of the call screen, the partner's side, exactly as at the table. The sun rim comes from upstage camera-left for both hosts. One sun lights both rooms, so the spread reads as one world.
- **What's behind:** a wall that isn't white, one plant in focus range and one far back, the practical glowing low in the background. No window edge, screen, logo, LED strip, shelf of crypto books or member swag anywhere in frame.
- **The mic arm** enters from the outer edge of frame (frame left for Bruce, frame right for Facundo), so nothing crosses between the two pages.

## 2. Recording

### Cameras and lenses

**One camera model everywhere: the Panasonic Lumix S5II.** It's full-frame, it records 10-bit V-Log internally, and it has a fan, so there's no overheating cut-out on a 45-minute take. It also has dual card slots and phase-detect autofocus. One model means one technical LUT and one set of menus for two hosts who aren't camera operators. Buy used bodies if you can; they are common.

**One lens family: Lumix S f/1.8 primes.** They share size, weight, filter thread and colour rendering, so cameras match without effort.

**Don't use phones or webcams as host cameras.** The look depends on a real 50 mm lens on a large sensor. The overhead is the exception: it has no faces and needs deep focus.

**Frame rate and the bumper.** The live action is 23.976 fps. The bumper is animated at 24 fps (brand kit, character bible). The animation studio delivers the bumper flagged as 23.976, with the same 120 frames. The music WAV is placed unchanged. At 23.976, frame 120 falls 5 ms after the music's 5.000 s button, an eighth of a frame, which nobody can see. Don't let Premiere retime a 24 fps file, which drops or doubles one frame in a thousand.

### Audio

Three rules come from the format. Speech is the whole product (no music under talk). Older ears lose consonants first (the Grandma test). And the real sound of props is used, never replaced (THEME §4). So:

- **Dynamic microphones, close.** 5–10 cm from the mouth, just below the chin line, angled up. They reject the room, which home offices need, and at the table they keep each host off the other's mic. Kept below the mouth so they never hide the face.
- **32-bit float recorders.** A laugh can't clip a 32-bit float file, and nobody is riding levels on a two-person show.
- **A room you've quietened.** A rug under the desk or table, soft furnishings, moving blankets on walls out of shot, heating and air conditioning off during takes.
- **Room tone:** 30 seconds of both hosts silent at the start of every session, in every room, with the room in the same state as during takes (THEME §5 needs it for the Still).

### The remote platform: Riverside

**Recommendation: Riverside** (riverside.fm), on the plan that includes separate WAV tracks per person and a producer seat [VERIFY the current plan name and price].

- **Why Riverside:** local capture is its core product. Each person's audio and video is recorded on their own machine and uploaded progressively, so a bad connection damages the call, not the recording. It has a producer seat for the editor, who can talk to the hosts without being recorded.
- **What it's for on this show:** the call itself, the producer's seat, a backup of every track, and a sync reference for the camera files.
- **What it isn't for:** the master picture. Riverside sees the S5II through an **Elgato Cam Link 4K** capture device, but the S5II's own card, in 10-bit log at 23.976, is the master. Set Riverside to 1080p; it's a backup, and that halves the upload.

|---|---|
| Browser | Chrome, on wired Ethernet, with every other app closed |
| Camera input | Cam Link 4K (the S5II's HDMI, showing the V-709 view) |
| Mic input | MV7+ by USB (Tier 1) or the MixPre-3 II by USB (Tier 2) |
| Recording | 1080p video; WAV 48 kHz audio; separate tracks |
| Noise suppression and echo cancellation | Off. The hosts wear in-ears, so there is no echo to cancel. Confirm at E00 that the local track records unprocessed [VERIFY]. |

**If Riverside fails on the day,** run the call on any video tool. The masters are local, so the recording is unaffected, and the editor syncs by the slate claps.

**Remote conversation needs half a beat more patience.** There is always some latency. Bruce still interrupts, but he lets Facundo land the sentence first. In the edit, the editor may tighten the pause between two turns. Nothing is ever compressed inside a sentence, and two voices are never overlapped to fake a quicker exchange.

### Backup discipline

**The rule of the card: a card is full until it is proven empty.**

**At the moment of recording, everything exists at least twice.**

| Source | Copy 1 | Copy 2 | Copy 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Each S5II | SD slot 1 | SD slot 2 (backup recording) | Riverside (remote only) |
| Each voice | Recorder SD (32-bit float) | Riverside WAV, or the laptop's Reaper copy in person | Camera scratch track |
| iPhone overhead | USB-C SSD | None. Facundo narrates every step (FORMAT §6), so the sound still carries the demo if the file fails, and the singles still show his hands. | – |

**At wrap, before anyone leaves:**

1. Offload every card with **OffShoot** (formerly Hedge), with **xxHash64** verification and an MHL manifest, to two destinations at once: the session SSD and the backup hard drive. Keep the card folder structure and the camera's own file names.
2. Put a strip of red tape on every card as it comes out of a device. It comes off only when step 4 is done.
3. Upload the episode folder to the shared **Dropbox** folder `MH_Incoming`. Remote hosts do this from home overnight; the one-page wrap checklist walks them through it.
4. The editor downloads, runs OffShoot's MHL verify against the manifest, and replies "verified E07 B-CAM card 1". Only then does the host format the card, **in the camera, never on a computer.**

**Cards needed:** four 128 GB V60 UHS-II SD cards per body (two in the camera, two in rotation), from Angelbird or ProGrade. One spare card per recorder.

**In the cloud:** the editor's working folder syncs nightly to a **Backblaze B2** bucket with **Object Lock set to 30 days**, so nothing can be deleted or overwritten by accident, or by malware, for a month.

**Keep all the raw footage, forever.** A season is about 2–3 TB, which costs a few dollars a month in B2 [VERIFY current price]. Light Work cuts from the back catalogue between seasons. And on a trust show, the raw is the proof: if anyone claims a quote was cut out of context, the full exchange is there.

**The project:** Premiere saves every 5 minutes, keeps 50 versions, and saves a backup project to Creative Cloud. Exports are never overwritten; each gets a new version number.

**Test a restore once a season.** Pick an episode at random, restore its folder from B2 to a clean drive, and open the project. A backup that has never been restored is a hope, not a backup.

### Consent

**Community questions read on air**

A question is read only with a clear "yes" from the person who asked it. A public comment isn't consent to being read on a show.

1. **The pinned comment** under every episode says [HOST GATE on the wording, FORMAT sign-off item 8]:
   > *"Answer below, first names only. We may read a few answers or questions on the show, word for word, with your first name. We'll always reply to ask you first, and we never read out money amounts or anything about your setup."*
2. **The ask.** Bruce replies publicly to the chosen comment (YouTube has no private messages):
   > *"Hi [name], we'd love to read this on Many Hands [NN], word for word, credited to '[first name]'. Is that OK? You can say no, or pick a different name. A reply by [date] works."*

   For a question from the contributors call or a community channel, Bruce asks the person directly in that channel.
3. **The log.** Every ask goes into `MH_CH_ConsentLog_v01.xlsx` with: episode, date asked, platform and link, the exact text as it will be read, the credit name, the answer (yes, no, no reply), the date of the yes, who asked, any edits or paraphrase, and a withdrawal date if there is one.
4. **No reply means no.** The backup question runs instead.
5. **Never read aloud:** amounts, balances, wallet or bank names, the number or identity of someone's helpers, a surname, a place, or anything that describes a setup. Paraphrase it, and Bruce says on air that he did (FORMAT §6).
6. **Minors:** if someone says they're under 18, or clearly is, the question may run, credited only as "a viewer".
7. **If someone withdraws consent after publishing,** the editor blurs the name on the question card with YouTube Studio's editor and removes it from the description and pinned comment within 7 days, and the log records it.
8. **Light Work uses the same consent.** A yes for the flagship covers a Short of the same answer; the ask says so.

**The hosts' own release** [HOST GATE]

Each host signs one appearance release per season with the DeRec Alliance, the legal entity that owns the footage [VERIFY the entity, as the music and character documents also require]. Counsel drafts it [VERIFY]. It covers:

- use of the recordings in the flagship, Light Work, trailers, podcast feeds and key art;
- **no AI-generated or AI-altered likeness or voice** of that host, anywhere, without a separate written consent for that specific use;
- **the off-the-record rule:** either host can say "off the record" during recording, and that stretch is cut, no questions asked;

### Edit rules: the trust rules

These keep the conversation real. They aren't style preferences.

1. **Cut talk, never a segment** (FORMAT §4). Never pad; a 15 that comes in at 12:40 airs at 12:40.
2. **Never change what someone meant.** An answer may be shortened. It's never reordered or spliced so that it says something the host didn't say.
3. **Reaction shots come from the same exchange,** within about 30 seconds of the line they react to, or they aren't used. A laugh lifted from another moment is a small lie.
4. **Hide a cut by cutting to another camera,** never with Morph Cut or any tool that makes new frames of a face (§10).
5. **Jump cuts are allowed in Light Work only,** where the format expects them.
6. **A wrong technical claim is fixed honestly.** It's cut if it adds nothing; otherwise it's corrected with a paper slip, a recorded pickup, or an IOU entry. It's never fixed with a synthetic voice (§10). A correction that changes meaning gets the Still (THEME §5).
7. **Where It Stops is untouchable** except for length: the spread or two-shot holds, no cutaways, no music, no mascot, the band per the brand kit.
8. **Filler words are cut by a person, one at a time.** No automatic silence or "um" removal across the conversation. The brief asks for unhurried, and automatic trimming takes out the pauses where two friends think.
9. **"Off the record" is cut, no questions,** and so is anything a host vetoes at Gate 3.

### The look: `MH_Daylight_709_v1`

The brand kit names the look and sets its targets. This is how it's built and used.

**Built once, from real footage, by a colorist.** On E00 footage from both setups, in DaVinci Resolve, in one day. The look is used for years, so it's worth one professional day.

| Target (brand kit §7) | How the colorist checks it |
|---|---|
| Highlights roll off to Paper `#F7F3E6` | The brightest non-specular highlight lands at about RGB 247/243/230. Nothing clips to pure white except glints on glass. |
| Shadows land on a green-black of about RGB 12/20/18 | Measured on the chart's black patch and on Facundo's black tee |
| Skin a touch warm, on the skin-tone line | The vectorscope, on both hosts, in both setups |
| Greens toward Jade's hue, about 170° | The plants. Never neon lime. |
| Blues toward Sky, never teal | The backdrop, and the sky in the window of any remote room |
| Gentle S-curve, saturation about 95% | Judged on a phone as well as the calibrated display |
| Never teal-and-orange, bleach bypass, bloom, or a vignette over 10% | Any vignette is added separately in the edit, never baked into the LUT |

**Three files, in this order on every shot:**

1. **The technical transform** from camera log to Rec.709: the manufacturer's own LUT, renamed. `MH_BR_Tech-VLog-to-709_v01.cube` (Panasonic's V-Log to V-709) and `MH_BR_Tech-AppleLog-to-709_v01.cube` (Apple's Apple Log to Rec.709). Applied as a **master clip effect**, so every use of a clip inherits it.
2. **Balance:** a per-clip Lumetri pass that matches exposure and white balance to that session's chart. This is matching, never restyling.
3. **The look:** `MH_Daylight_709_v1.cube` (33-point), in the Creative Look slot of a Lumetri instance on an **adjustment layer** over the host footage only. One instance for the whole episode, at 100%. It keeps the brand kit's name exactly, and the brand kit versions it.

**The adjustment layer never covers the bumper, the end card, full-frame cards or thumbnail text.** Those are already display-ready Rec.709 and would be graded twice.

**Lock it on a phone.** Before the look is locked, the E00 test stretch is uploaded privately to YouTube and watched on an iPhone, an Android phone and a laptop. The look is locked only when it holds on all three. Judge the grade in the YouTube player, never in QuickTime, which shows Rec.709 brighter than YouTube does.

### The mix

| Track | Chain | Level |
|---|---|---|
| A1 Bruce, A2 Facundo (mono) | High-pass at 80 Hz; gentle EQ; compressor at 3:1; de-esser. Premiere's DeNoise only if the room demands it, at 20% or less. | Dialogue sits at about −14 LUFS short-term (THEME §5) |
| A3 table mic (Tier 2, in person) | High-pass at 120 Hz | Under the voices, up for the jar and the tokens |
| A4 room tone | Unprocessed | Fills every gap and the whole of the Still |
| A5 music | The composer's files, from `02_MUSIC`, at unity, placed from the master files every time | THEME §5 table |
| A6 sound effects | S01–S03 and the episode's activity sound | THEME §5 table |
| A7 camera scratch | Muted | Sync only |

**Master:** Premiere's Loudness Meter on the mix bus, targeting **−14 LUFS integrated (±0.5)** and **−1.0 dBTP**. Premiere's Hard Limiter isn't a true-peak limiter, so set it at −1.5 dB and confirm the true peak with the meter. The podcast master is the same mix with 2 dB less gain, at −16 LUFS (THEME §6).

### Captions: human-corrected, never auto-only

Captions matter more on this show than most: the Grandma test, and the fact that YouTube's auto-captions garble "DeRec" and "Shamir" (FORMAT §2).

1. **Draft.** Premiere's Speech to Text on the fine cut, set to at most 42 characters a line, at most two lines, at least 24 frames per caption, and a 2-frame gap between captions. *Editor.*
2. **Correct.** The editor plays the whole episode against the draft and fixes every line. About 2.5 hours for 15 minutes. The standard:
   - **Clean verbatim:** what was said, minus false starts and "um"s. Never reword. Keep "Kundo" when Bruce says it.
   - **At most 17 characters a second.** Slower than the usual 20, for Grandma. Split long captions at natural phrase breaks.
   - **Speaker names** in capitals ("FACUNDO:") only when the speaker is off screen or it's unclear who's talking.
   - **Sound tags** in square brackets, sparingly: `[theme music]`, `[token clinks into the jar]`, `[laughter]`. No tag in the Still; silence doesn't need a caption.
   - **The glossary** (`00_ADMIN/MH_Glossary_v01.txt`): DeRec, `lib-derec`, Shamir's Secret Sharing, Dr. Leemon Baird, DeRec Alliance, Many Hands, Light Work, Tuck, Pip, and every member name exactly as the member writes it (Algorand, Cardano, Hedera, Ripple, MultiversX, Kabila, BankSocial). Search the file for every glossary term after correcting.
   - **Numbers:** words for one to nine, figures from 10. Version strings exactly as on the Hands On label.
3. **Proof.** A second person, never the corrector. Bruce watches the fine cut with captions on at Gate 5 and marks every error on Frame.io. Facundo searches the caption file for the technical terms only, which takes ten minutes.
4. **Deliver.** Export SRT and upload it to YouTube as English. After processing, spot-check three random points in the YouTube player.
5. **Reuse.** The corrected SRT becomes the Light Work burned-in captions and the podcast transcript.

**If corrected captions aren't ready at lock, the Premiere moves.** The show never goes public with only YouTube's automatic track.

## 10. AI in the pipeline

The show is about trust, and the protocol's inventor is wary of AI (brief §6). So the line is simple enough for anyone to remember: **AI may help people do the work faster. It never makes something the audience will take for the hosts, their voices, the characters or the music, and it never decides what's true.**

### Where it helps

| Job | Tool | What the software does | What a person does | Ships? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Transcripts** | Premiere Speech to Text, which runs on the editor's machine [VERIFY on-device processing in the installed version] | The first draft of the transcript | The editor fixes speaker names and glossary terms. Facundo reads the transcript for Gate 2, which is faster than watching. | No |
| **Rough cuts** | Premiere's text-based editing | Turns selected transcript text into timeline cuts | The editor chooses every cut, and listens to every one | No |
| **Caption drafts** | Premiere Speech to Text | Timing and first-draft words | The full correction pass and a second person's proof (§4) | Yes, corrected. Named in the "How we make this" line. |
| **Look development** | An image generator, for environment-only mood frames | Quick explorations of palette and light for the brand kit's targets | The colorist builds the LUT by hand, from real footage and the chart. No generated frame is ever shown to the audience. | No |
| **Vertical reframes for Light Work** | Premiere Auto Reframe | Proposes crop positions | The editor checks and adjusts every shot. It crops real pixels and invents none. | Yes, as a crop |
| **Noise cleanup** | Premiere's DeNoise and DeReverb | Removes noise and room sound | The editor sets amounts, and never above 20% on a voice | Yes, as cleaned real audio |
| **Bar-one plates** | Only if both hosts choose it (R9) [HOST GATE] | A draft painting of the episode's world | A matte painter finishes it. It never includes the ritual, the characters, the stones or any lettering. | Yes, with YouTube's label and a description line |

### Where it must not go

1. **The hosts' likeness.** No generated or altered faces or bodies, anywhere in an episode or a Short, even with consent (FORMAT §3). That rules out:
   - AI thumbnails, and AI "enhancement" of a face;
   - Premiere's **Generative Extend**, which invents new frames of a person;
   - **Morph Cut**, which synthesises frames of a face to hide an edit;
   - eye-contact correction, AI retouching and face super-resolution;
   - background blur or replacement on the set.

   Outside episodes, in key art, a generated likeness is possible only with both hosts' written consent for that use, and disclosure (brand kit §7, route 3) [HOST GATE]. **The reference stills in `assets/reference/` are AI-generated likenesses of the hosts.** They stay internal unless both hosts approve a specific public use, and any such use is disclosed. [HOST GATE]
2. **Voices.** No cloning, no text-to-speech, no "regenerate" or "fix a word", no AI dubbing, no voice for Tuck or Pip. Speech enhancers that rebuild a voice rather than clean it, such as Adobe's Enhance Speech, are off too, because at strength they change how a person sounds. A fluffed word is fixed by a cut or by a real pickup.
3. **The ritual master, and every character master.** Tuck, Pip, the paws, the stones and the threads are made by hand, and the ritual is animated once and locked (R4, R9). The shield comes from the Alliance's master file and is never generated; no generator renders any letter or logo (brand kit §3).
4. **Music and sound.** No generated music, no AI extension or rearrangement of the theme, no generated activity sounds (THEME §7, R9). The composer's stems cover every edit need.
5. **Facts.** No AI tool is a source for a technical claim, the fact sheet, a myth verdict, a chapter title or a description line. Facundo checks against the spec. People write every word the audience reads.
6. **The community's data.** Nobody pastes an asker's details, or unpublished footage, into a cloud AI service. Transcription runs on the editor's machine.
7. **Editorial judgement.** No automatic silence removal, "best take" selection or auto-edited Shorts. Every cut is a person's decision (§4).

### Disclosure practice

- **A standing line** in the channel's About section and in every description says how the show is made (the "How we make this" paragraph in §7). It names the humans, and it names the software that drafts captions. [HOST GATE on the wording]
- **YouTube's altered or synthetic content label** is ticked whenever AI-generated or AI-altered picture or sound ships in that video. On this show, that means an AI-assisted plate. The description then adds one plain line, such as: *"The painted background in this episode's opening was drafted with [tool] and finished by [artist]."*
- **The label is not ticked for workflow AI** (transcripts, caption drafts, crop suggestions), because nothing synthetic ships. The label tells viewers that the picture or sound is synthetic. Ticking it for captions would tell them something false, on a show whose whole job is telling the truth. The brief's rule is still met: those uses are disclosed plainly, in the standing line. [HOST GATE on this reading of brief §6]
- **The AI log.** Every episode's `MH_E07_AILOG_v01.md` lists each tool, its version, what it did, and who checked the result. Bruce signs it at Gate 4.
- **If a disclosure is missed,** it's fixed within 24 hours: the label ticked, the line added, and a pinned comment saying what changed and why. A correction made in the open costs less trust than one found by a viewer.
