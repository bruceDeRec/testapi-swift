# Creative director's rulings

*These rulings close the concept phase. Every document built after this point follows them. Where a foundation document (FORMAT, BRAND-KIT, THEME-AND-SOUND) disagrees with a ruling below, the ruling wins, and the build phase updates that document.*

---

## R1. The mascot is Tuck, a sea otter, with Pip as her friend

**The panel's scores** (average of four independent judges): Tuck 76 · Pip 75 · Dee the martlet 61 · Loop 59.

| Lens | Winner | Tuck's score |
|---|---|---|
| Charm and repeatability | **Tuck** 90 | 90 |
| Metaphor truth and brand safety | Loop 86 | 76 (second) |
| Production craft | Pip 86 | 50 (last) |
| The client's taste | **Tuck** 88 | 88 |

**Why Tuck.** She won both lenses that decide whether people *love* the show: charm, and the client's own taste. She came second on truth. Her one weak lens, craft, is a cost problem, and cost problems have known fixes (R4). A charm problem doesn't. She's also the only concept that answers the brief's north star directly: AI Code King's dancing pandas are a round, lovable animal doing one joyful thing to a strong hook. Tuck is that, grown up. She has button eyes, adult competence, and a real bit of anatomy as her signature: the loose skin pocket under a sea otter's forearm.

**Pip stays, as Tuck's friend.** Pip is a mug-sized porcelain café robot built on the DeRec-shield silhouette, with one bending face-line. This answers the client's "DeRec bot / DeRec helper" line, and it guarantees the *agentic positive future*: a small machine doing small, kind things. Pip appears in the ensemble activities: chess, the bell choir, repotting, the lantern dance, and a two-step as a tip of the hat to the dancing pandas. Pip keeps its own movement rules: height is mood, the face-line bends but never sweeps, and it makes a ceramic "tink" when something lands on its flat top.
**Two friends, side by side, is the show's motif.** Tuck and Pip echo Bruce and Facundo. Nobody says this out loud.

**Pip never holds a stone.** A robot holding someone's share would teach "an AI keeps my keys." That is false, and it's the misreading most likely to upset the protocol's inventor, who is wary of AI. Pip is Tuck's friend, never a helper.

**Retired, with thanks:**
- **Loop / Ting**, the gold cord that snaps into shape once enough beads land on it. This is the truest picture of Shamir's scheme. It moves into the teaching segments as **the Curve** diagram: an ink line on paper that wobbles through every possible shape while it has fewer points than the threshold, and snaps to exactly one shape at the threshold. It is not the mascot and not the gold light thread. See R6.
- **Dee**, the heraldic martlet. Her two-note whistleable idea is already met by the theme's hook, which is built on the rhythm of the proverb.

## R3. The ritual (bar two of the bumper) is locked forever

In order:

1. **The hush.** Drums and bass drop out a few frames before the downbeat of bar two. This is Pip's rule, grafted onto Tuck.
2. **The pocket pat.** Tuck turns her face to the lens and pats her left pocket once, on the downbeat.
3. **The stone.** She draws out her plain, matte grey stone in both paws and holds it up to the lens. Nothing about it glows.
4. **Two more stones.** Two otter paws slide in with their own plain stones. They come from fixed frame edges, never show a face, and never touch each other. Which two friends' paws appear rotates quietly between episodes, because any enough will do.
5. **The threshold.**
   - The share sound is identical for every stone: *plink… plink…*
   - At the third stone there is a held breath, then the **home chord**, and all three stones light amber at once, like the gems on the café table in reference 04.
   - One stone never glows. Two stones never glow.
   - The threshold must hold for **at least 12 frames** so a phone viewer can see it. The craft judge found 8 frames too few.
6. **The release.** The amber light lifts off the three lit stones as two gold threads. The threads rise out of Tuck's world and meet in the middle of the title page, which is the brand kit's title-card move. Tuck never touches a thread after it forms. This is the only bridge between the mascot and the light thread, and it resolves the brand kit's rule that the mascot never touches the thread.
7. **The tuck.** The paws withdraw the way they came. Tuck tucks her stone away: *pat-pat*. One slow, content blink, then the scene melts away, leaving the title page on the sky. *Amended by the storyboard: the title page sits in the sky above Tuck for the whole ritual, and the melt ends on the brand kit's title card, not on paper. See `intro/COLD-OPEN-STORYBOARD.md` §3.*

**Non-disturbance rule.** Each episode's carry-over detail survives the whole ritual untouched. That might be a maple leaf on her head, a baton behind her ear, or flour on her nose. The ritual is so routine it disturbs nothing, and that's what keeps it funny on the 60th viewing.

## R4. Craft fixes, required

These address the craft judge's objections:

- **Fur** is sculpted and hand-painted, with no groom simulation.
- **Effects** (splash, snow, steam, flour, leaves) are 2D cards animated on twos. Never simulated.
- **Backgrounds** are matte-painted 2.5D plates, **lush and saturated like the client's references**. Don't desaturate them. Tuck stays readable by rim light and depth of field, not by greying the world.
- **The ritual master is animated by hand once, rendered once, and locked.**
  - The carry-over detail is a separate tracked layer composited on top, so nothing is re-rendered per episode.
  - Only the defocused background plate behind the ritual changes.
- **Per episode, the new work is** about 2.5 seconds of activity animation, one plate, one carry-over layer, and one activity sound effect.
- **Every plate carries exactly one "tomorrow" detail:** an air taxi, an autonomous shuttle, a crystalline skyline, or a café robot. That guarantees the touch of modernity.
- **Tuck's Year.** Each episode's activity matches the month it airs, so the regulars' season matches hers.

## R5. Timing: the music is the clock

- The theme runs at **96 BPM in D major, two bars of 4/4**, ending exactly at **5.00s**, where the edit cuts to the hosts.
- One beat is 15 frames at 24 fps. Bar one (0.00–2.50s) is the activity. Bar two (2.50–5.00s) is the ritual.
- The storyboard owns the final hit-point table and edits `music/THEME-AND-SOUND.md` so the two agree. No other document edits the music's timing.

## R6. Teaching graphics are not the brand thread

- The gold light thread means **people connected by care**. The brand kit keeps it out of teaching segments.
- Teaching uses **the Curve**: an ink diagram on paper that wobbles freely while it has fewer points than the threshold, and snaps to one shape at the threshold. It's the honest picture Facundo can defend line by line.
- The limits Facundo states on camera go with it:
  - the secret is one number, not the whole curve;
  - real shares live in modular arithmetic, so there's no smooth line to look at;
  - the smooth curve is the classroom picture.

## R7. Words

- No spec word ("helper", "share", "owner", "secret") is ever used as a mascot's name or label on screen.
- Tuck is Tuck. Pip is Pip.
- The hosts may say, once, in Episode 01: *"Tuck's a cartoon. Your helpers are real people you choose."*
