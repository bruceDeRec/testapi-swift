## How the slate was built

### The pillars run in a fixed rotation

FORMAT's rule says any three consecutive flagships must cover all three pillars. That rule leaves one pattern. If episodes 1–3 and episodes 2–4 both hold all three pillars, episode 4 must repeat episode 1's pillar. So the season rotates:

**What if → Who you trust → How it works**, four times.

- Episode 01 is *What if*, because that pillar opens every season (FORMAT §2).
- Episode 02 is *Who you trust*, because Episode 01's Next Time recommends the question "Who should you trust to help you get back in?"
- So *How it works* lands on 03, 06, 09 and 12. It never runs twice in a row, and Grandma never gets two mechanism episodes back to back.

### Features alternate strictly

Hands On runs on odd episodes (01, 03, 05, 07, 09, 11). Heard It Somewhere runs on even episodes (02, 04, 06, 08, 10). The finale uses FORMAT's 20-minute version, which runs both.

**Real software on screen is a [HOST GATE].** Episode 01's script keeps every Hands On on the table until Facundo has a build he's happy to show. The slate plans the first real-software demo for **Episode 09**, where it pays off the season's first IOU. If a build is ready sooner, Episode 05 is the first candidate. If it isn't ready by 09, 09 runs on the table and the IOU moves to the clinic.

### Lengths

- **Episode 03 runs FORMAT's 10-minute version.** Its question has a yes-or-no answer.
- **Episode 12 runs the 20-minute version.** It's the clinic.
- **Everything else runs the 15-minute standard.** Publish short if the answers come in clean (FORMAT §4).

### The loop runs two episodes ahead

The playbook records episode N+2 in each call week and keeps one finished episode in reserve. So each episode's pinned question feeds the Raised Hands of the episode **two** ahead, plus the call-week Light Work in between. Each pinned question below was written for the episode it will actually feed.

One kink: Episodes 05 and 06 both record on 15 December, before Episode 04 airs. So Episode 04's thread feeds Light Work on 15 January instead, plus the clinic's backlog. Episode 06's Raised Hands comes from the 14 December contributors call or from Episode 03's thread.

## The season arc

**Someone who watches all twelve understands six things they didn't before:**

1. **What they'd actually lose.** Mostly the way back in, not the things. Keys versus things (01, 06).
2. **How the helper idea works, in plain words.** A few people you choose each hold a share that means nothing on its own. Enough of them together bring your secret back. They don't know about each other. Your phone checks on them quietly every day (01, 03, 09).
3. **How to choose helpers, and how to be one.** Pick people who'd spot a fake and won't share your bad day. Tell each one only about themselves. As a helper, hand your share back only when it's really them (02, 05, 07, 10).
4. **How to have the conversation.** With a parent, with a child, at the family table (02, 11).
5. **Exactly where it stops.** Each of the four limits is learned in the situation where it matters: a hack (01), a scam (03, 10), a payment you approved (11), and looking after your own devices (06, 07). Each one comes with what does handle it. The clinic says all four together, once.
6. **Who's behind it.** An open standard designed by Dr. Leemon Baird and built in the open by an alliance of organisations, none of which owns it (08).

**The shape.** Episodes 01–04 (Nov–Dec) draw the picture. Episodes 05–08 (Jan–Feb) test it against people and bad days. Episodes 09–12 (Mar–Apr) prove it on camera and hand it to the audience. The loop pays off along the way: the IOU from 01 is paid in 09 or the clinic, Percent Right grows up into Say It Back, the Jar is counted, and the recipe's secret ingredient is finally revealed.

**The twelve One Small Things add up to a checklist** a viewer can finish in two hours across a season, with or without DeRec.

## The contingency for a missed fortnight

**A missed fortnight means a flagship can't premiere on its Wednesday.**

### First defence: the buffer

The playbook records two ahead and keeps one finished episode in reserve.
- **A lost recording day** (a host is ill on the call-week Tuesday): record later that week, remote if need be. The lock is 13 days away.
- **A lost recording week:** record on the show-week Tuesday. That's FORMAT's original 8-day turnaround, and it still makes the date. Rebuild the buffer with a double session at the next call week, as on 15 December.

### If a slot is lost anyway

This covers a gate that fails, a fact Facundo can't confirm, or both hosts out.

1. **Never pull the next episode forward.** The previous Next Time promised this episode's exact title. The pillar rotation would break. And every bumper is tied to its date.
2. **Never fill the slot** with a rerun or a clip compilation posing as a flagship.
3. **On the Wednesday, Bruce posts a community post** in plain words: what's late, why (in one line), and the new date. On a trust show, you say it.
4. **Friday's Light Work runs from the bank.** The channel still speaks every week.
5. **The late episode premieres two weeks later,** on the next Wednesday of the grid. Every later episode slides two weeks, and the finale moves to Wed 28 Apr.
6. **Tuck's activities stay on their dates** (bible rule 1). Each episode takes the bumper of its new date. The missed date's bumper is shelved for the same month in season two. **The lantern dance goes with the finale** to 28 Apr. The date it leaves, 14 Apr, takes the season-two spring activity *roller skating*. Holiday lines drop if their episode slides past the holiday.
7. **Bible edits if this happens:** Tuck's Year dates from the missed slot onward; the production companion rows that follow them; season two starting 12 May; roller skating moving into season one; New Stones following the changing-helpers question to its new date (never into season one).
