# Tuck and Pip: character bible

*The binding reference for everyone who draws, models, animates, lights, scores, edits or merchandises the characters of* Many Hands*. It implements DECISIONS R1, R3, R4, R7, R9 and R10–R13. Where this bible and DECISIONS disagree, DECISIONS wins. For any frame number, `intro/COLD-OPEN-STORYBOARD.md` wins. For colour tokens, `brand/BRAND-KIT.md` wins. **[HOST GATE]** marks something that needs Bruce's or Facundo's sign-off. **[VERIFY]** marks a fact to check before anyone relies on it. Both are listed at the end.*

*Frames are counted from f0 = 0.00s, as in `music/THEME-AND-SOUND.md`, at 24 fps. One beat at 96 BPM is 15 frames, so the beats fall on f0, f15, f30, f45 (bar one) and f60, f75, f90, f105 (bar two). The cut to the hosts is f120 = 5.00s. The concept panel counted from f1; ignore its frame numbers.*

## The characters on one page

- **Tuck** is a grown sea otter in storybook 3D. She spends her day off doing one thing beautifully, and every cold open ends with the same ritual: she pats the pocket under her left forearm, holds up the plain grey stone she keeps there, and when two unseen friends bring theirs, the three stones light amber together.
- **Pip** is Tuck's friend: a mug-sized, speckled-porcelain café robot with one bending line for a face. Pip joins her for eight of the first 24 activities. **Pip never holds a stone** and never appears in the ritual.
- **The camera is the owner.** Tuck shows the stone to the lens because she keeps it for whoever is watching. Only the camera sees all three stones.
- **The ritual is locked.** It is animated by hand once, rendered once, and never re-rendered. Per episode, the new work is about 2.25s of activity, one plate, one carry-over layer and one activity sound.
- **Tuck's Year.** Each activity matches the month it airs. The 24 activities below run from THE FLOAT on 11 November 2026 to a tandem ride with Pip on 29 September 2027.
- **Neither character is ever a label.** Tuck is Tuck and Pip is Pip. No spec word (owner, helper, share, secret) is ever used as a name or on-screen label (R7).
- **The best object is the plush:** Tuck with a real pocket and a grey stone inside it. Nothing is sold until the clearance steps at the end are done. [HOST GATE]

# Part one: Tuck

## Name

**Tuck.** Four reasons, in order of weight:

1. **It is the verb of her signature move.** The ritual ends with *the tuck*: she tucks her stone away, pat-pat. Regulars learn her name by watching her do it.
2. **"Tuck away" means to keep something safe and out of sight.** That is exactly what she does with the stone, and it promises nothing beyond that.
3. **"Tucked in" is warm.** It carries the cosy, unhurried feeling the brief asks for.
4. **It is one syllable,** easy to say for English and Spanish speakers, and it is not a spec word (R7).

Known associations are harmless (Friar Tuck, the novel *Tuck Everlasting*). A trademark knockout search runs before any merchandise or registration. [VERIFY] [HOST GATE]

## The character statement

> **Tuck is a grown sea otter who spends her day off doing one thing beautifully, and who keeps one small, plain stone for you without ever asking what it is for.**

Every piece of animation is checked against that sentence. If a shot makes her less grown, less good at the thing, less calm, or more curious about the stone, it is off-model.

## Five adjectives, and what each rules out

| Adjective | What it looks like | What it rules out |
|---|---|---|
| **Unflappable** | When something goes wrong she smooths her cheek fur and carries on. | Panic, flailing, fear takes, "oh no" faces, sweat drops, storms, predators, anything chasing her. |
| **Competent** | She does each hobby like a master craftsperson on her day off. The comedy comes from commitment and scale. | Pratfalls played for pain, failure as the punchline, being the butt of the joke, clumsiness gags. |
| **Quietly delighted** | Delight lives in a whisker lift, a cheek squint and one flap of a hind flipper. | Mugging, winks, open-mouthed grins, fist pumps, celebration dances outside the two dance episodes, playing to camera. |
| **Discreet** | She keeps the stone without looking at it and shows it only in the ritual. | Looking at the mark or the threads, examining another stone, looking at the friends' paws, showing the stone off, peeking in her pocket. |
| **Grown-up** | Adult posture, adult timing, button eyes. | Baby proportions, saucer eyes, a pup sidekick, bows, a voice, tantrums, toddler-show pacing. |

## The back-story (the team knows it; the audience never hears it)

None of this is ever said, captioned or shown as exposition. It exists so every animator makes the same choices.

- **Where she lives.** A small sunlit cove below the white terraces of the reference city, where the waterfalls come down to the sea. Her kelp scarf comes from the bed off her cove.
- **Her age.** Mid-life. Grown, capable, not old. The cream on her head and throat is real: sea otters go pale around the head as they age.
- **Her work.** She restores old wooden dinghies at the glass marina. That is where the competence comes from, and why she handles everything with two careful paws. She takes every other Wednesday off. Nobody on screen ever mentions that this is also when the show premieres.
- **The stone.** Long ago, someone asked her to keep one small, plain thing that meant nothing on its own. She said yes and did not ask what it was for. She keeps it in the pocket under her left forearm, the one she never uses for anything else. She takes it out only when she is asked.
- **Whose stone it is.** Yours. In the team's fiction the owner is whoever is watching, which is why she shows it to the lens. The team never decides who "you" are, because the answer is every viewer.
- **The chip.** Her stone has one tiny chip on its edge. It was there when she was given it. It is how she, and the regulars, know it is hers.
- **The pat.** Patting the pocket is a habit, like patting your coat for your keys. It means "still got it". It is care, never worry.
- **The friends' paws.** They belong to your other friends, not hers. She has never met them, which is why she never looks at them and they never touch.
- **Pip.** Pip works at the terrace café above the cove. One windy morning Pip found her kelp scarf caught on the café railing and brought it down to her, folded. They have spent days off together ever since. Pip does not know what the stone is for and has never asked to hold it. Pip has never been asked to keep anything for anyone, and never will be.
- **Why two friends.** Tuck and Pip, side by side, echo Bruce and Facundo: the one who tries anything and the one who does it exactly. Nobody says this out loud (R1).
- **Why "she".** Female southern sea otters use tools more than males (Law et al., *Science*, 2024) [VERIFY]. The show's hosts are two men, and its third character should not make it three.

### Silhouette rules

1. **A bean with a disc on top.** At any size she reads as a round head on a full, soft body. Never lanky, never long-necked.
2. **The home pose is the logo pose:** floating on her back, head up, paws together on her chest, hind flippers up at the far end. It must read in solid black at 48 px.
3. **Only whiskers and hind flippers break the outline at rest.** A carry-over item may break the crown, within the limits in "The carry-over layer".
4. **Short arms are the species and the comedy.** Forelimbs never stretch past 0.8H. To reach something, she moves her whole body.
5. **Big feet, small hands.** The hind flippers stay oversized in every pose.
6. **The pocket shot is a three-shape stack:** head, jade band, stone at the chin. That stack is the character's second logo.
7. **Every new pose passes the black-silhouette test** before it is animated. If it doesn't read as an otter in silhouette, re-pose it.

**Contrast, measured.** Cream on cocoa is 7.67:1, so her face reads in greyscale. Jade on cocoa is only 1.75:1, which is why the scarf sits between the cream face and the cream bib (4.38:1), never on the brown fur. Slate on cream is only 2.42:1, which is why the stone carries a heavier contour line (70%) than the rest of her. Amber on slate is only 1.32:1 in luminance: **the threshold reads by the Filament core and the Gilt halo, not by the amber body alone.** Never ship a lit stone without its core.

**Banned on Tuck:**
- **Blue of any kind,** including the brand kit's Sky, Zenith and Horizon. Otter.ai uses an otter identity, and the show's protocol inventor is wary of AI (R9).
- **Glass `#7BE1DA`.** Cyan belongs to machines. It is Pip's colour.
- **Red.** The Episode 01 maple leaf is scenery on her head, not part of her.
- **Any member organisation's colours.**
- **Gold, anywhere except the lit stones and the threads.**
- **Pure white and pure black.**

### Materials

- **Fur: sculpted, never simulated** (R4). Clumps are carved into the mesh and hand-painted, with clump-edge highlights in the albedo and a fresnel rim shader in `#E3BE8F` for the peach fuzz. It gives felted-toy warmth for a fraction of the cost of a groom.
- **Tuck is always modelled dry.** Even afloat, her fur keeps its dry-look clumps. Water shows only as a 2D waterline sheen card and 2D ripple cards. This keeps one fur asset for every episode, and it means bar one never breaks continuity with the locked ritual.
- **Nose, paw pads and inner ears:** soft subsurface scattering with a warm red-brown scatter.
- **Eyes:** glossy, one catchlight, no iris detail.
- **Kelp scarf:** waxy and slightly translucent (jade subsurface), with a faint ribbed specular.
- **The stone:** matte and dry (roughness 0.85), in every shot. It appears only in the ritual, and the ritual is always dry.
- **The lit stone:** emissive Amber with a Filament core and a soft internal gradient, like the café gems. **No cut facets.** A faceted gem says treasure. The stone keeps exactly its own shape when lit.

### Rendering and the contour line

- **Stylised 3D, with the character animated on ones** at 24 fps.
- **Effects** (splash, snow, steam, flour, leaves, ripples) are **2D cards animated on twos**, never simulated (R4).
- **The threads** are hand-drawn 2D **on ones** (R12): 12 drawings, f102–f113, travelling at near-constant speed (about 79 px a frame along the path) with a 2-frame ease at each end, not the brand kit's `draw` curve. Their shape may wobble by 1 px or less between drawings. Their brightness never changes: no flicker, per the brand kit. They are visibly a different medium, so the magic never reads as biology.
- **The NPR contour line: the "printed book" line.**
  - Ink `#123E32` at 50% opacity.
  - 2 px on the shadow side and 1 px on the light side, at 1080p.
  - Drawn on the silhouette and on major form breaks: the pocket crease, the forelimb over the chest, the scarf edges, the flipper edges.
  - No line on colour boundaries (the edge of the cream mask is colour, not line), and none on the whiskers.
  - On the stone: 70% opacity, so it separates from the cream bib in greyscale.
  - Dropped entirely on the avatar and icon versions.
- **Light, every shot:**
  - a warm golden key, high from camera left;
  - a cream bounce;
  - jade-tinted shadows;
  - a cool sky fill (Horizon `#CFE6F6`), which gives the brand kit's "warm people, cool sky";
  - and **always a rim light.** Tuck stays readable by rim light and depth of field, never by greying the world (R4).
- **Bloom only on gold:** the lit stones and the threads.
- **No paper grain on the moving image.** Grain costs bitrate and turns to mush on YouTube, as the brand kit says of its furniture.
- **Plates** are matte-painted 2.5D cards of the reference world, **as lush and saturated as the client's references** (R4). The brand kit's "10–15% below the references" note is for key art and does not apply to the bumper plates. Remove only the references' HDR faults: halos, crunchy leaves, plastic sheen.
- **Touchstones:** the painterly 3D of *The Wild Robot*, lit like a Beatrix Potter watercolour.

### The stone and its chip

**Tuck's stone: a smooth, flattened egg of matte slate.**

| Property | Spec |
|---|---|
| Shape | A flattened egg, 0.6H × 0.42H × 0.22H. The broad end sits to camera left when she holds it up. |
| Surface | Matte, dry, roughness 0.85 |
| Colour | Slate `#8E948F`, underside `#6F7571` |
| Speckles | 14 pale speckles in `#B7BBB5`, about 2% of the surface, in a fixed pattern painted once and locked in the asset. Plain speckles, never a pattern, picture or glyph. |
| **The chip** | One crescent-shaped chip on the rim at **two o'clock** as she holds it up to the lens, 0.06H across and 0.02H deep, showing the paler interior `#A9ADA8`. It always faces the camera in the ritual. It still shows as a notch in the outline when the stone is lit, so the lit stone is still visibly hers. |
| Held | Always in both mittens, always two-handed, the ends of the stone showing past her paws |
| Lit | Only at the threshold, and only when three stones touch. Never alone, never in pairs. |

**Stone rules for the whole show:**
- **The stones are never coin-shaped.** No flat discs: every stone's thickness is at least 50% of its width.
- **Never stamped, engraved, faceted or patterned.** A mark on a stone would teach that a share carries part of the secret. It doesn't.
- **Never gold at rest.** Never glowing at rest.
- **Never jigsaw-shaped.** No stone's edge ever fits another's.
- **Never used as a tool.** A real otter's stone is a hammer or an anvil. Tuck's never hits anything, because smashing reads as breaking in.
- **Only in the ritual.** No stone, pebble, bead, marble or gem appears anywhere else in her world. Fixed scenery rock (cliffs, terrace walls, boulders) is fine. Anything loose and stone-shaped near her is not.

### What makes her read at 48 px

Four shapes: **a cream disc** (the face), **two dark dots and a nose**, **a jade band beneath**, and **a grey oval at the chin** (the stone). The avatar and icon versions drop the contour line, sit on an Ink `#123E32` circle, and are tested in greyscale at 48 px and 24 px before sign-off. Everything in the ritual is composed inside the centre 9:16 area. Shorts never crop the 16:9 master: the *Light Work* stamp is its own native 1080×1920 pass, rendered once (R13).

## The expression sheet

Her mouth never opens. Every expression is built from six controls: **lids** (percent closed), **brow mounds** (the raised cream fur above the eyes), **cheek mounds**, **whisker angle** (0° is relaxed back), **head tilt** and **hind flippers**. The eyes never change size.

| # | Expression | Controls | When it's used |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Home: content** | Lids 15%. Whiskers relaxed, 0°. The mouth line turns up very slightly at the corners. | Her default. The first frame of the ritual. Any hold. |
| 2 | **Absorbed focus** | Lids 30%. Brow mounds pinch in 0.03H. Whiskers forward 15°. Head down 5°. Hind-flipper toes curl. | The moment of doing the thing: the brushstroke, the chess move, the potter's wheel. |
| 3 | **Curious** | Lids 0%. Head tilts 12°. Whiskers forward 20°. Brow mounds lift 0.02H. | Inspecting something: the maple leaf in Episode 01. |
| 4 | **Mild surprise** (never startled) | Lids 0% for 4 frames. Whiskers flare forward 25°. Head draws back 0.05H. Recovers in 6 frames. | A gust, a splash, the maple leaf landing over her face in Episode 01. Never fear. |
| 5 | **Quiet delight** | Cheek mounds lift, closing the lids 10% from below. Whiskers rise 20° and fan. One flap of a hind flipper. | When it goes right. At most twice per activity. **Also once in every ritual, in a locked form (R10):** over f91–f94, as the stones light, the whiskers lift and the cheeks rise so her eyes smile. No flipper flap, no head move, paws still, eyes on the lens. Then she holds. |
| 6 | **Not quite** | One brow mound lowers 0.02H. A mouth corner flattens. A short nose puff moves the whiskers. Then the grooming tell: she smooths her right cheek with her right paw. | A small setback, recovered with dignity. |
| 7 | **Pride** | Chin up 5°. Lids 20%. Whiskers back and up 10°. A cheek smooth as a flourish. | After the button of an activity. Also carries her **one dry glance to the lens**, when she takes it: eyes to the lens without turning the head, held 8 frames. At most once per bumper, in bar one only. |
| 8 | **The content blink** | A slow blink over 8 frames: close 3, hold 2, open 3. Whiskers ease back. The mouth line softens. | **The ritual only.** It closes every episode, so it is never used anywhere else. |

**Never, in any expression:** an open mouth, teeth, a tongue, a wink, a scowl, a frown, tears, blush marks, sweat drops, sparkle eyes, heart eyes, comic raised brows, or any look at the mark, the threads, or another stone.

## The movement bible

### Weight on land: denser than she looks

- She **settles.** When she stops, her body keeps going for 2 frames and sinks 0.03H into the ground contact, then recovers over 4 frames.
- **The waddle:** a reusable cycle of one step per eighth note (7–8 frames). Her body rolls 6° side to side, hind flippers slap flat on contact, the tail drags. It is built once and reused in every land episode, because flippered animals walk awkwardly and one good cycle is cheaper than many poor ones.
- She **sits back on her tail** to use both paws, like a real otter.
- Flipper slaps are the only contact sounds on land (see "Sound").

### Weight in water: lighter than air

- She **bobs** 1.5% of her body length over 30 frames: two beats, so the water breathes with the music.
- She **sculls** with her hind flippers, alternately, one stroke a beat.
- She **rolls** onto her back over 10 frames, spine first, head last.
- Water is painted: a 2.5D displacement plate plus 2D ripple and splash cards on twos. No fluid simulation, ever.

### Timing

- **Every major action lands on a beat** (f0, f15, f30, f45 in bar one).
- Anticipation: 3–4 frames. Holds: 6 frames minimum, so a phone viewer can read them.
- **Whiskers drag 2 frames** behind the head. **The scarf tail and float bulb drag 3–4 frames.** Both are hand-keyed or on a simple spring chain. No cloth simulation.
- **Hands lead with care.** Anything precious is held in both mittens, and the body follows the paws by 2 frames.
- **The double beat.** Every bar-one button contains a two-part beat (two flipper kicks, two pats of the soil, two taps of a bell) that foreshadows the ritual's pat-pat. Regulars start to hear the ritual coming.
- **Bar one ends looking to frame left.** Her last beat of activity resolves with her eyeline toward frame left, so the ritual's head turn at f54 reads as her turning from what she was doing to us.

### The grooming tell

A quick smooth of the right cheek with the right paw, over 8 frames. It means both "that went well" and "I'm fine". It is her punctuation: at most once per activity.

### What she never does

- Runs upright on her hind flippers, tiptoes, or struts.
- Points with a single digit, waves, bows, gives a thumbs up, or claps for applause. (She has mittens. The one exception is the baking episode, where she dusts two oven mitts together, which is a practical gesture, not applause.)
- Throws, skips or taps any stone.
- Dives underwater. Her world is the surface.
- Lunges toward the lens, or moves toward the camera faster than 0.1H a frame.
- Falls in a way that would hurt.
- Moves while the threshold holds (f90–f101), with one exception: the locked quiet-delight reaction over f91–f94 (expression 5), whiskers and cheeks only. Otherwise nothing moves then but the glow settling.

## Sound

**No voice, ever.** No words, and no otter vocalisations either: no squeaks, chirps, whistles, coos, sneezes, giggles or voiced yawns. A voice decides too much about a character, and a synthetic one would sit badly on a trust show (FORMAT §7).

**Her foley.** All real recordings, never generated, in the brand's family of glass, wood and felt, never metal or electronics (THEME §4).

| Sound | How it's made | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **The pat** | A palm in a felted-wool mitten patting a felted-wool coat over the chest. Dry, unpitched, 80–120 ms. | The ritual: once on the downbeat (f60), then twice at the tuck (f107, f112). The same sample every time, locked in the master. |
| **The plink** | One river pebble set against another, close-miked, plus a faint vibraphone A5 ring 12 dB under (the tonal layer of THEME's S01). | The ritual: each time a friend's stone touches. **Identical for every stone.** Only its pan changes. Never a different pitch, because a share isn't a piece of the secret. |
| **Flipper slap** | A flat, wet palm on wet sand | Land contacts in bar one. Two per activity at most. |
| **Water** | Real lap and ripple recordings | Water episodes. Never a big splash unless the activity is the splash. |
| **Grooming smooth** | A soft brush across velvet, about 150 ms | With the grooming tell |
| **Nose puff** | One dry, unpitched exhale, 4 frames | Expression 6 only. At most once per episode. |

**The activity sound** (THEME's A-*nn*): one per episode, 0.50s at most, real, on a beat of bar one, at least 6 dB under the hook, and tuned to D major pentatonic (D E F♯ A B) if it has pitch. The downbeat of bar two now belongs to the pat and the group clap, so the activity sound moves to beat 2, 3 or 4 of bar one. Each episode's is listed in Tuck's Year.

**The sonic signature of the ritual:** *pat* · *plink* · *plink* · (a held breath) · **the home chord** · *pat-pat*. It can be tapped on a desk. The ritual's foley (pat, plinks, pat-pat) is delivered as one locked stem, `RIT`, alongside the music stems.

## The ritual, frame by frame

**Locked forever (R3).** Animated by hand once, rendered once, and never re-rendered. Only the defocused plate behind it and the carry-over layer change per episode (R4).

### Staging

- **One locked-off shot: the pocket shot.** Chest-up, with the lens at her eye height. She faces the lens. No camera move: the ritual is still, and the only motion is Tuck, the paws, the light and the threads. A still camera also keeps the carry-over tracking trivial.
- **Framed at mid-chest,** so it works whether she was afloat or on land in bar one. Nothing in the frame says which.
- **She sits in the lower 45% of the frame** (y 594–1008 at 1080p), below the title page, as the brand kit's title card reserves. Her crown sits at y 670 and her head centre at y 788 (storyboard §3). The stones meet at about y 912, centred on x 960, inside the 9:16 safe area.
- **The plate** behind her is a defocused derivative of that episode's bar-one plate, warmed 300 K. Its sky seam sits at **y 640, feathered over y 616–664**, so the descriptor's baseline (y 592) sits on clean sky (R13). Every ritual plate must accept the master's warm key: night episodes use a lamplit or lantern-lit plate, never a cold moonlit one.
- **The two friends' paws enter from two fixed points on the bottom edge, at x 700 (left) and x 1220 (right), rising about 170 px to the stones.** A forearm plus paw of 0.8H (about 190 px) spans that with the friends' bodies out of frame below; from the side edges, about 850 px from the stones, no otter paw could reach. Both entry points sit inside the centre 9:16 area (x 656–1264). Not the top right, where the concept panel put one: the title page and the shield live in the top of the frame, and a paw there would cross them.

## The carry-over layer

## The "never" list

Tuck never:

1. Speaks, sings or vocalises.
2. Opens her mouth, shows teeth or tongue, winks, scowls, cries or panics.
3. Eats, or holds food to her mouth. No crabs, shellfish, urchins or fish in her world: sea otters eat them, and there is no prey on this show.
4. Uses her stone as a tool, hits anything with it, throws it, or handles any other stone, pebble, bead, gem or coin.
5. Shows her stone outside the ritual, or looks at it inside the ritual (the New Stones head is the one exception, and it is her old, spent stone).
6. Looks at the mark, the threads or the friends' paws, or touches a thread.
7. Guards, fights, chases or rescues anything. No storms, predators (orcas, sharks, eagles), thieves, locks, vaults or alarms. Nothing that implies recovery stops hacks, scams or theft, and no fear-selling.
8. Appears in a raft of otters holding paws around stones. A chain of linked helpers is exactly what DeRec avoids. Rafts may appear only as scenery, far off, and never with stones.
9. Appears with blue, with meeting or transcription imagery, or with a microphone treated as a product (Otter.ai, R9).
10. Appears next to a crab of any kind, including Ferris, the Rust mascot. The community will make that joke for us.
11. Wears a shield patch or any logo, or holds, wears, sits on or replaces any part of the lockup.
12. Appears next to a member organisation's name, logo or colours, money, a price or a chart.
13. Appears while a limit, risk or failure is being stated, in a demo, on a thumbnail, or in the first 60 seconds after the bumper (FORMAT §7).
14. Is labelled with a spec word, or called anyone's helper, on screen or in copy (R7).
15. Is petted, hand-fed, held or "owned" by a person. The southern sea otter is listed as threatened [VERIFY], and the show never models that.
16. Visits space, the deep sea or a fantasy set piece. Her activities are things a person could do on a good day off.
17. Has a pup sidekick, or shares a frame with another Tuck.
18. Is generated by AI in the ritual master (R9).

# Part two: Pip

**Pip is Tuck's friend, never anyone's helper.** A mug-sized porcelain café robot built on a softened version of the DeRec shield's outline, with one bending line for a face. Pip answers the client's "DeRec bot" line and guarantees the agentic positive future: a small machine doing small, kind things (R1).

**The name.** A pip is a seed, a small point of light, and the short beep of a radio time signal: small, bright and punctual. One syllable, easy in English and Spanish. It is also the name of Python's package installer, and Facundo gets the dry line about it if it ever comes up. Trademark check before merchandise. [VERIFY] On screen and in copy, Pip is only ever "Pip". The concept panel's "Pip, the DeRec helper" is retired (R7).

## Construction

**The unit is P, Pip's body height: 18 cm in-world, 0.9 of Tuck's head height.**

**Silhouette.** A softened shield: a flat top with rounded shoulders, tapering to a blunt point. One piece: no head, no neck, no legs. It hovers, so it never needs a walk cycle. The flat top works like a tray, a nod to the cover's coffee-serving robots, and things land on it. Two separate mitts float beside it.

## Movement rules

1. **Height is mood.** Pip floats up when pleased (up to 0.3P above its rest height), sinks when concentrating (down to a 0.05P hover gap), and drops 0.1P and recovers over 6 frames when startled.
2. **Tilt is attention.** No neck, so Pip leans its whole body, up to 12°, toward whatever it cares about.
3. **Mitts lead, body follows.** The mitts arrive 2–3 frames before the body and settle 2–3 frames after it.
4. **Stillness before contact.** Two frames of complete stillness before Pip touches anything. (The same idea became Tuck's hush.)
5. **The idle bob:** 2% of its height over 60 frames, one bar, so it breathes with the music. The bob slows when Pip concentrates and stops completely when Pip sits in a vehicle or on a chair.
6. **Rigid, always.** No squash, no stretch.
7. **Pip is animated on ones,** like Tuck.

## The face-line

Four shapes, and only four:

| Shape | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **—** | Focus, and deadpan |
| **·** | Curious, or startled |
| **~** | Effort |
| **^** | Content |

- **The line bends, but never moves side to side.** A sideways sweep reads as a scanner, and this show has no surveillance.
- **"^" appears once per appearance,** at the button of the activity, so Pip's happiest face lands on the same kind of beat every time.
- **Pip looks at the lens at most once,** and only with "^", which reads as eyes closed, so it never stares.
- **Every Pip appearance includes one "—" beat.** The deadpan is Pip's humour.

## The tink

- **When anything lands on Pip's flat top, it makes a ceramic tink.** A real recording of a teaspoon tapping a porcelain cup rim, dry, 0.3s at most, **tuned to D6** (D major, so it never clashes with the theme).
- **Why D6 and a cup:** the stone plink is slate on slate with an A5 ring. Pip's tink must never be mistaken for the share sound, so it differs in both material and pitch.
- **The deadpan beat:** something lands (tink), the face-line goes to "·" for 8 frames, then "—", and Pip carries on.
- **Pip makes no other sound.** No voice, no hover hum, no beeps, no chirps. The brand's sound palette is glass, wood and felt, never electronics. Its mitts touch things silently.

## Pip never holds a stone

This is R1's hardest rule, because the misreading it prevents ("an AI keeps my keys") is false and is the one most likely to upset the protocol's inventor.

Pip never:
- holds, touches, carries, catches, guards, stands beside or looks at a stone;
- appears in the ritual, or in any frame with the stones or the threads;
- has a window, pocket, compartment, drawer or tray that could hold something for someone;
- glows amber, or holds anything amber;
- is handed anything by the friends' paws;
- "checks in", pats itself, or performs anything resembling the ritual;
- is called a helper, or keeps anything for anyone, in the fiction, in copy or in merchandise.

In the badminton episode a shuttlecock lands on Pip's top and Pip flips it back. It is a toy, it is feathered, and it goes back over the net. That is the limit of what Pip ever "holds".

## New Stones

**What it is.** The one sanctioned variant of the ritual, for the episode about changing helpers. In DeRec, the owner's app re-shares automatically when helpers come and go. The helpers do nothing (brief §5). New Stones shows that honestly: Tuck doesn't swap anything herself. She finds a new stone already in her pocket.

**Exactly when it is used.**
- **Episode 13, Wednesday 28 April 2027, the season-two premiere.** Its question is about changing helpers. Working question: *"What happens when one of your helpers drops out?"* The season-two slate sets the final wording.
- **It follows the question, never the slot.** If the season-two slate moves the changing-helpers question to another episode, New Stones moves with it, and Episode 13 runs the standard ritual.
- **Never in season one.** Season one teaches the stable picture first.
- **At most once a season** from then on, and only on an episode whose question is about changing helpers or re-sharing. Never for spectacle.

**How it runs.** The New Stones master is the standard master with six frames added at the front, so that episode's activity has 48 frames (f0–f47). Episode 13's activity, the washing line, was chosen because it is simple enough for 48 frames.

| Frames | Time | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| f48–f51 | 2.00s–2.17s | Cut into the pocket shot. Tuck is holding her **old stone**, now pale sand `#CDBF9F` and dull, its chip still at two o'clock so regulars know it was hers. She glances down at it: the only time she ever looks at a stone. |
| f52 | 2.17s | She lets it drop out of the bottom of the frame. Real otters do this ("they use a rock, and then they drop it"). Silent. Nobody picks it up. |
| f53 | 2.21s | Her paws return to her chest and her head rises into the standard master's first pose. |
| f54–f119 | 2.25s–5.00s | **The standard ritual, frame for frame,** except: the stone she draws from her pocket at f61 is her **new stone**, the same flattened-egg shape with its chip at **ten o'clock**; the two paws are **Fern and Gran**, both carrying their new-shaped stones; and Sandy's paw is gone. |

**What changes permanently.** From Episode 13 on, Tuck's stone is the new one, every friend's stone has a new shape, Sandy has left the rotation and Fern has joined it. Nobody's new stone repeats any season-one shape, because a new share is not an old share handed on. All of this was rendered in the initial build, so Episode 13 costs no re-render.

**What the hosts say about it: nothing.** The metaphor is explained once, in Episode 01. Regulars who notice will say it in the comments, and that is better than telling them.

**Honesty check.** It pictures two claims:
- that re-sharing gives every helper a new share;
